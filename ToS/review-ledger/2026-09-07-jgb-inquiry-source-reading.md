## What the source supports, and what the researcher constructs

Section 19 contrasts the apparent unity of the word for willing with a complex
of feelings, thought and commanding affect. It describes command, expected
obedience and appropriation of successful execution. The new experienced-agency
Aspect isolates that dimension of the already described conception. It does not
create another conception or certify the account as empirical psychology.

Section 21 connects a maximal metaphysical demand for responsibility with
self-origination and attacks that conception. It also warns against simply
substituting a mechanistic unfree will. The described Position is limited to
this double refusal. It is not a complete or timeless authorial position.
The Opposition names the two notions discussed there, preserving the warning
that their opposition does not establish exhaustive alternatives or warrant
accepting one after rejecting the other.

The same section treats cause and effect as conceptual conventions for
designation and communication and criticizes their reification. The new
PhilosophicalCategory describes this passage's treatment of causality. It is
neither a technical cause-relation type nor an ontological commitment imposed
on the ToS core. Describing this account does not adopt its truth or the section's
further claims about people, character or society.

The two Problems, their ProblemFamily and the Questions are explicit
researcher formulations: the tension between word unity and experienced
complexity in section 19, and the relation between ultimate responsibility,
self-origination and the limits of its rejection in section 21. They are not
claimed as verbatim questions or an authorial taxonomy. The family groups these
distinct inquiries by their bearing on agency and responsibility; it does not
identify them as one problem or one argument.

The Distinction comparing experienced freedom with metaphysical self-origination
is also a researcher comparison across the two passages. It differentiates
their explanatory tasks without claiming logical incompatibility or historical
development merely from section order. Separate term Claims identify exactly
which conceptions are compared. The second Question addresses the tempting
reversal to unfreedom; its proposed positive answer remains the existing
explicitly hypothetical thesis, connected to the already addressed objection.
