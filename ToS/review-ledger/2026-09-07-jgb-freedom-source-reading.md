## Observed distinctions and proposed reconstruction

Section 19 treats willing as a complex of feeling, thought and commanding
affect. Its account of experienced freedom involves a relation between command
and obedience and the willing person's attribution of successful execution
to the will. The present Conception describes that particular account, not
an accepted psychological theory or the author's whole philosophy.

Section 21 attacks a metaphysical understanding of ultimate self-responsibility
by connecting it to self-origination and characterizing the latter as
self-contradictory. The proposed argument reconstruction makes this connection
explicit as two premises and a concluding rejection. This is a reconstruction
of the passage's reasoning, not a formal proof supplied by Nietzsche or ToS.
The metaphysical conception is the *target* of the criticism, not a position
silently endorsed by the author because he articulates it.

The same section cautions against simply replacing that conception with its
mechanistic opposite. The objection record therefore targets the specific
inference from rejecting metaphysical freedom to accepting an unfree will.
That inferential target is an explicitly hypothetical reconstruction of a
reading the passage warns against; no historical person is said to have made
it. The objection does not erase the first argument or assert that all senses
of freedom are equivalent.

The crosscutting Freedom referent is a research organizing commitment spanning
questions of freedom, agency, constraints and responsibility. Its continuity
criterion allows rival and critical accounts without declaring a single shared
definition. The relationship between these accounts is proposed by explicit
membership Claims, not inferred from spelling. They are not dated stages in
Nietzsche's changing beliefs merely because they occupy different sections.
