## Inspected sources and limited readings

- [Oxford: Akkadian](https://www.orinst.ox.ac.uk/akkadian), opening description: language tradition and
  Semitic classification. It is not a language identification of every artifact.
- [ETCSL: Sumerian language](https://etcsl.orinst.ox.ac.uk/edition2/language.php), Context: attribution as an isolate,
  temporal/spatial variation and uncertainty of spoken-language cessation.
  Phonemes and the morphemic example demonstrate that transliteration is not
  phonological reconstruction or a one-sign/one-morpheme mapping.
- [Ira Spar, The Origins of Writing](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/the-origins-of-writing), October 2004:
  the paragraph on wedge-shaped signs grounds the broad writing-tradition account.
  This introductory essay does not settle one universal inventory or origin date.
- [Unicode 17, §7.1 Latin](https://www.unicode.org/versions/Unicode17.0.0/core-spec/chapter-7/): Latin script use and extensions.
  Encoding coverage is not a language, historical proof or transliteration rule.

## Boundaries and exclusions

The existing Penn and Louvre artifact IDs are retained. Their older native
metadata remains unchanged, including its own inscription observations. New
Claims record only selected source reports, not an automatic metadata migration.
A museum period does not identify an inscription's dialect. Streck's account
supports separate dialect and historical-stage relationships for the variety;
neither is an identity equality or a transitive rule.

Russian descriptions and claim statements are newly authored qualified accounts;
English names and content are their declared source metadata, not faithful
translations of ancient Akkadian or Sumerian. No ancient-language competence is
claimed. A scheme is not a transformed text, an alignment or a translation;
the Latin-based notation Claim leaves digits and editorial symbols explicit.

All subjects remain provisional and all Claims unreviewed/uncertain. Source
review/admission, source-copy form readiness, valid mechanics and ToS canon remain
distinct. No images, full copyrighted texts or corpus payload were acquired.
Only brief factual paraphrases, bibliographic references and minimal catalog
values are carried; source-page access does not authorize payload redistribution.
