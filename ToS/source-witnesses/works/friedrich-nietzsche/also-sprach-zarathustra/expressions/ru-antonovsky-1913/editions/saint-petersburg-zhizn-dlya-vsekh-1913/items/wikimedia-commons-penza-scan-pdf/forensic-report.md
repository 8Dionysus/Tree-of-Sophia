## Source-visible review

PDF pages 7, 13, 15, and 402 were rendered and inspected.

- Page 7 names Friedrich Nietzsche, the work, translation by
  Yu. M. Antonovsky, Saint Petersburg, and 1913.
- Page 13 begins the translator's preface. It says that earlier versions of
  the translation appeared across editions in 1900, 1903, 1907, and 1911 and
  that they were substantial reworkings rather than simple reprints.
- Page 15 marks the start of part one.
- Page 402 is the closing cover surface.

Wikisource describes the 1913 witness as a fourth edition. The visible title
page does not state an edition number, while the translator preface names four
edition years ending in 1911. ToS therefore preserves the edition-history
question instead of inferring that the 1913 printing is textually identical
to a named earlier edition.

## Rights posture

The Commons description declares this exact digital object public domain under
its `PD-RusEmpire` rationale and separately states United States
public-domain status for publication before 1931. This is stronger positive
evidence than mere open availability.

It is still imported source evidence, not a jurisdiction-complete ToS legal
judgment. The operator-held payload remains local and is not promoted for a
future-site upload. A separately served public copy may be considered only
after an exact terms and jurisdiction refresh.

The Wikisource page text is offered under CC BY-SA 4.0 with notice that
additional terms may apply. That licensed transcription is not admitted as
source text: the inspected landing and page surfaces expose an unresolved
proofreading posture.
