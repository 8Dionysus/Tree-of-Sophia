## Critical text: wording matters

The [eKGWB 1886 correspondence](https://www.nietzschesource.org/texts/eKGWB/BVN-1886),
letter 705, identifies Constantin Georg Naumann as recipient. Its dateline names
Naumburg an der Saale and 3 June 1886. Nietzsche acknowledges a cost calculation,
accepts the paper, and requests accelerated printing with at least three sheets
completed weekly. He explains the urgency by a planned larger summer project
and the freedom he says he needs for a new field of thought. These are requests
and a self-report, not proof of the actual production rate or a causal explanation
of the subsequent philosophy.

The letter does not name the book in the inspected wording. Its connection to
Jenseits and the characterization as an immediate work order come from
[Sommer 2016, p. 6](https://digi.hadw-bw.de/view/nietzsche_kom5_1/0026).
The latter remains an attributed scholarly interpretation, not a verbatim
statement of the letter. Reading this text narrows the earlier claim's evidence:
acceleration and initial commencement must not be treated as interchangeable.
No contradiction about the date has been found in these inspected sources.

## Archival identity and representations

The Klassik Stiftung Weimar / Goethe- und Schiller-Archiv
[correspondence record 4752](https://ores.klassik-stiftung.de/ords/f?p=406:2:::::P2_ID:4752)
was inspected through its public search and detail interface. It catalogs this
letter under **GSA 71/BW 291,1, leaf 8**, one leaf, dated 3 June 1886, sent from
Naumburg to Leipzig. It separately notes a receipt mark and a comment attributed
to Naumann. Its printed-edition reference is KGB 3.3, p. 193, no. 705.

The digital viewer exposes two sides, `8vs` (image record 9722) and `8rs`
(9723). Both rendered images were viewed. The front shows the location and
date; the reverse shows the continuation. This is bounded visual inspection,
not a complete diplomatic transcription, attribution of every added mark, or
physical inspection of the original. No image payload was downloaded, republished
or declared locally fixed. The stable citation above is preferable to a
session-bearing viewer URL. Public viewing does not establish reuse permission.

The recipient display associates Naumann with a Leipzig printing establishment;
that does not make the person, firm, correspondence role and material carrier
one identity. Nor do the printed edition, its digital critical text, the archive
description and the two images become four independent historical testimonies:
their dependence on the same underlying letter remains material.
