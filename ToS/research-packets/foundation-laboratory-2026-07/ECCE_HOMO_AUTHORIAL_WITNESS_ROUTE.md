# Ecce Homo: Authorial Witness and Critical Route

Status: current research route, not admitted source text, author-final
reconstruction, accepted German, publication permission, or semantic truth

Research date: 2026-07-30

Discovery receipt:
`ToS/source-witnesses/discovery/runs/ecce-homo-authorial-witness-route.2026-07-30.v1.json`

## Result

The stronger foundation beyond the 1908 Insel witness is now located, but it
is not one object and it is not a simple line from “original” to “copy.”
*Ecce Homo* survives as a responsibility- and time-layered witness family:

```text
late compositional notebooks and loose leaves
  -> Nietzsche's print-ready manuscript D 25
    -> Köselitz/Peter Gast print-ready copy D 25a
      -> corrections, proofs, replacement passages, deletions, and lost leaves
        -> posthumous editorial publications, including the 1908 Insel witness
          -> Colli/Montinari critical reconstruction with stable eKGWB addresses
```

This diagram is a route, not a claim that every edge is direct, complete, or
uncontested. Some stages overlap. One important Nietzsche manuscript is lost;
one copy survives in Peter Gast's estate; one three-leaf deletion/change file
is cataloged but not digitized. The durable ToS foundation is therefore the
exact identity and provenance of each witness plus versioned claims about
their relation. It is not a synthetic “author-final” text assembled by a
model.

## Research order

The route was checked in the required order:

1. current official archive, edition, access, and rights documentation;
2. established historical-critical commentary;
3. current manuscript chronology and the freshest directly relevant
   philology;
4. general web only as a final lead surface, with every imported claim
   reopened at its originating record.

No manuscript images, critical-edition body, or restricted article were
downloaded into ToS. No access request or paid photo order was sent.

### What is stable here

- current shelfmark and ORES record identity;
- manifest URL, current canvas count, and source-declared license field;
- recorded extent, classification, and hands;
- the fact that a record is digitized or not digitized;
- the exact date and source of each metadata claim.

### What remains a versioned interpretation

- whether a hand is authorial, copyist, editorial, or later annotation at a
  particular region;
- the chronological order of individual inscription layers;
- which changes represent Nietzsche's final intention;
- the directness and direction of copying relations;
- whether a reconstructed critical reading should be preferred for a
  particular passage.

## 3. Established scholarly baseline

Andreas Urs Sommer's
[historical-critical commentary, volume 6/2](https://doi.org/10.11588/diglit.70914)
is the established bridge between archive identity, publication history, and
the Colli/Montinari reconstruction. It:

- maps D 25 to current `GSA 71/32`;
- separates Nietzsche's print manuscript from the Köselitz copy;
- documents the replacement-section and family-editorial history;
- explains why the 1908 Insel publication cannot stand in for a critical or
  author-final text.

This established layer corrects the tempting but false shortcut:

```text
1908 first publication == Nietzsche's final text
```

The 1908 item remains valuable as an exact publication and reception witness.
It is not the authority for reconstructing the last authorial state.

## 4. Current and freshest relevant scholarship

### Manuscript chronology

Beat Röllin's 2024
[Chronologie der Manuskripte 1885–89. Nachtrag zu KGW IX](https://doi.org/10.1515/nietzstu-2024-0001)
provides the current chronological supplement for distinguishable inscription
layers in the late manuscripts. It is especially important methodologically:
ink, copying relations, paper, handwriting, correspondence, and writing
direction can support dating, but many dates remain intervals or plausibility
judgments rather than exact instants.

For the *Ecce Homo* route, the current scholarship supports at least these
stages:

1. an early compositional state in W II 9;
2. an October state;
3. a first print manuscript around mid-November;
4. a second print manuscript around early December;
5. proof corrections;
6. additions and replacements in late December 1888 and early January 1889.

These are scholarly chronological claims over physical inscription layers.
ToS must store their source, confidence, and version rather than turn the
sequence into an unqualified date field.

### Paratext and the status of the work

Wolfram Groddeck's 2025
[“lisez: Nietzsche”](https://doi.org/10.1515/nietzstu-2024-0035)
treats *Ecce Homo* as completed yet philologically and compositionally
fragmentary, and makes paratext relevant to how Nietzsche reads and presents
himself. This matters for future semantic work: title pages, headings,
self-quotation, and editorial framing cannot be discarded as scan furniture.

The article also demonstrates why recent scholarship is evidence, not
automatic authority. Its figure captions repeatedly cite D 25 as `GSA 71/31`.
Current ORES identifies
[GSA 71/31](https://ores.klassik-stiftung.de/ords/f?p=401:2:::::P2_ID:75104)
as D 24, *Dionysos-Dithyramben*, while ORES, Kalliope, and HAdW agree on
D 25 = `GSA 71/32`. ToS preserves the mismatch as an unresolved scholarly
signature conflict and uses the current owner record for routing.

### Replacement section and revised dating

Beat Röllin's 2025
[Post für Nietzsche Dionysos](https://doi.org/10.1515/nietzstu-2025-0024)
grounds the surviving Köselitz copy of the lost replacement for
*Warum ich so weise bin* 3. It argues from a surviving preliminary draft that
the lost Nietzsche text was composed in early January 1889 rather than at the
end of December 1888.

The correct ToS representation is not to overwrite the archive catalog. It is
to retain:

- the GSA record's description of the section Nietzsche sent to Naumann;
- Köselitz's surviving copy and its estate provenance;
- the lost status of Nietzsche's corresponding manuscript;
- Röllin's newer dating claim with its argument and publication date;
- the earlier dating as a supersedable scholarly claim.

### A false “2026 frontier” corrected

William A. B. Parkhurst's
[Ecce Homo – Notes on Duplicates](https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110688436-006)
is a relevant semantic lead about Nietzsche's non-identical self-quotations.
The current publisher surface can show a 2026 copyright/download stamp, but
the bibliographic record identifies it as a chapter in a 2022 book. It is
therefore not fresh 2026 philology. Its full text remains restricted, only the
abstract was consulted, and no access control was bypassed.

This correction is itself part of the research method: a current webpage date
is not necessarily a work's publication date, and “2026” is not evidence of
freshness.

## Consequences for the ToS model

### Do not create a false local item

The current ToS `Item` contract requires a locally held, fixity-bound payload.
This research acquired no manuscript or critical-edition payload. Creating an
Edition/Item tree merely because an IIIF manifest exists would claim custody
and fixity that ToS does not have.

The correct current surfaces are:

- one schema-valid discovery receipt with exact remote identifiers;
- this non-authoritative research synthesis;
- a work record pointing to both;
- provenance for the discovery event;
- zero new payload, item, transcription, anchor, accepted-text, sign, or
  semantic records.

If ToS later acquires one exact authorized image or archive-supplied
reproduction, it can create an Item only after:

1. exact source and canvas/leaf identity;
2. immutable byte acquisition and digest;
3. archive and digital-object rights review for the intended scope;
4. responsibility and hand description;
5. no-publication default for the operator-held copy;
6. a separate public route only if explicitly permitted.

### The graph must represent alternatives

Future claims should be able to express:

```text
witness A copied_from witness B
witness A may_copy_from witness B
region R attributed_to Nietzsche
region R copied_by Köselitz
region R deleted_in state S
region R replaces region Q
chronology claim C dates region R to interval T
critical reading K reconstructs_from witnesses {A, B, ...}
publication P follows copy A with editorial changes
```

Every edge requires evidence and claim provenance. A current scholarly
re-dating should supersede or compete with an earlier claim, not mutate the
physical witness.

## Laboratory route opened, not scheduled

This research makes a future small A/B/C comparison possible without creating
routine human labor:

- **A — publication witness:** exact 1908 Insel page region;
- **B — critical route:** matching stable eKGWB section;
- **C — authorial route:** one exact D 25 or other relevant manuscript
  leaf/region, only after rights and acquisition scope are reconciled.

A valid comparison must freeze one concrete question first: a disputed
reading, deletion, replacement, responsibility boundary, or paratextual
change. It must not compare whole books indiscriminately. German linguistic
acceptance still needs declared competence; the Russian-speaking solo
operator can judge source identity, visible layout, provenance, and workflow
quality without being asked to certify unseen German grammar.

No Human Gold task, transcription exercise, or workbench run follows merely
from this route. Human review should occur only when one concrete decision can
change source admission, translation, a sign, a relation, or publication.
