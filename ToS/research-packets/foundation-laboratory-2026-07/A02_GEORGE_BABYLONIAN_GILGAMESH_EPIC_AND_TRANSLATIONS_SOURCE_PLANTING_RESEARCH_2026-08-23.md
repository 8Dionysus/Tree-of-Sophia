`Andrew R. George, The Babylonian Gilgamesh Epic и переводы`, described there

1. Andrew R. George's 2003 two-volume specialist critical edition,

## 2. Established and field-shaping scholarship

Eleanor Robson's 2004 BMCR review treats the two-volume edition as a major
critical publication after the long interval since Thompson, explains its
specialist cuneiform-facing purpose, and distinguishes it from George's more
accessible translation:

- <https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/2004/2004.04.21>

Alhena Gadotti's 2005 review supplies an independent bibliographic control for
the two-volume shape - volume I `xxxv, 741`, volume II `iii, 743-986`, and 147
plates - without making the inaccessible review body a ToS source payload:

- <https://doi.org/10.1017/S0041977X05260056>

Nathan Wasserman's 2011 review article, *The Distant Voice of Gilgameš*, is a
further reception and circulation control:

- <https://www.jstor.org/stable/24595100>

George's methodological essay explains that a critical edition is a
philological reconstruction grounded in vocabulary, grammar, syntax, and the
available manuscripts. It is not a final literary interpretation and cannot
be promoted as the ancient Work itself:

- <https://eprints.soas.ac.uk/3316/1/GilgameshGenre.pdf>

The established conclusion is therefore narrow: George 2003 is the necessary
historical critical control for this branch, not immutable ground truth. The
reader-facing George translation is a recognized translation witness, not a
substitute for the critical apparatus or for source-visible variants.
