## 1. Classical and official documentation

### The institutional collection and its internal groups

The current Yale Peabody Museum
[*Babylonian Collection*](https://peabody.yale.edu/explore/collections/yale-babylonian-collection)
page describes the collection as founded in 1911, formally affiliated with the
Peabody's Division of Anthropology in 2017, and holding more than 45,000
tablets and other materials. It identifies Sumerian, Akkadian, and Hittite
literary, legal, administrative, medical, and commemorative texts, alongside
seals and other objects. It also states that research access is arranged
through the curatorial division.

The collection's own current
[*Holdings*](https://babylonian-collection.yale.edu/about/holdings) page makes
an essential identity distinction. The umbrella includes six major groups:
YBC, NBC, MLC, GCBC, NCBT/NCBS, and RBC, plus smaller collections. Therefore:

```text
held by the Yale Babylonian Collection
  != catalog number must begin YBC
  != original collection, acquisition history, or provenience is known
```

Future object records must preserve the literal catalog prefix and number.
YBC, NBC, MLC, GCBC, NCBT, NCBS, and RBC identifiers cannot be normalized into
one fabricated YBC namespace. Institutional custody, subcollection history,
archaeological provenience, and ancient find context remain separate claims.

### Founding history is a sequence, not one magic date

Yale's official [Beginnings](https://babylonian-collection.yale.edu/beginnings)
history records J. Pierpont Morgan's 1909 offer, Albert T. Clay's appointment,
the rapid acquisition of more than 14,000 items, and the formation of the
research and publication program. The Yale Provost's 2017
[institutional memorandum](https://babylonian-collection.yale.edu/sites/default/files/provost_ybc_memo_5.12.17.pdf)
places the Laffan professorship in 1910 and reports about 8,000 objects by
1915. The current Peabody page uses 1911 for the collection's founding.

ToS keeps these as role-bearing events in an institutional history, not as a
single timeless creation date. The acquisition history also requires critical
provenance study; custody at Yale does not by itself establish archaeological
provenience, lawful modern chain of title, or ancient textual context.

### Exact identity of the 2019 catalog Work

The official Peabody
[*Exhibition Catalogs & More*](https://peabody.yale.edu/explore/publications/exhibitions-catalogs-more)
page gives the full title, editors Agnete W. Lassen, Eckart Frahm, and Klaus
Wagensonner, ISBN `9781933789378`, paperback availability, and distribution by
Yale University Press. The collection's official
[*Publications*](https://babylonian-collection.yale.edu/publications) page
records New Haven, Yale Peabody Museum, 2019. Current Yale editor profiles
independently confirm the editorial responsibility.

The companion catalog contains thematic scholarly essays and a catalog of
more than one hundred artifacts, some published there for the first time. The
physical [exhibition record](https://babylonian-collection.yale.edu/outreach/exhibits)
reports more than 150 objects and dates the Peabody exhibition from 6 April
2019 through 30 June 2020. Those are different scopes: catalog entries,
exhibited objects, and the institutional collection cannot be inferred to
have identical membership.

WorldCat supplies OCLC `1099939906`, ISBN-10 `1933789379`, and an issuing-body
route for the Peabody Museum. Provider descriptions disagree on physical
extent: publisher-facing metadata commonly reports 296 pages, while the 2023
JAOS review describes `xiii + 306`. No Edition or Item is created to hide that
manifestation-level discrepancy. A future acquired copy must transcribe its
own title page, copyright page, pagination, dimensions, and checksum.

### Rights are item- and use-specific

The current Peabody
[*Terms of Use*](https://peabody.yale.edu/about/terms-of-use) distinguish
public-domain materials from copyrighted and third-party content, require
users to inspect rights information on each object record, and retain rights
in the web compilation unless stated otherwise. The Museum welcomes legally
permitted fair use and says files may be downloaded for personal or
educational use subject to record-specific restrictions. This does not create
a blanket redistribution, model-training, or publication licence.

The publications page explicitly directs reproduction requests for Museum
titles to the publications manager and other museum-material permissions to
`permissions.peabody@yale.edu`. This is a prepared future channel, not a
message sent by this run. No catalog PDF, book scan, object image, virtual-
exhibition payload, or catalog-data export is acquired or admitted.

## 2. Established and field-shaping scholarship

### Foster 2013: institutional history is also collecting history

Benjamin R. Foster's
[*Albert T. Clay and His Babylonian Collection*](https://babylonian-collection.yale.edu/sites/default/files/files/Clay%20%26%20Babylonian%20Collection.pdf),
in *Beyond Hatti: A Tribute to Gary Beckman* (Lockwood Press, 2013), supplies
the established history of Clay, Morgan, the early acquisitions, and the
publication program. It is important not only for prestige but for provenance
pressure: the modern scholarly collection was assembled through historically
specific patrons, dealers, deposits, purchases, gifts, and institutional
transfers. Those routes cannot be replaced by today's holding label.

### Geller 2020: a strong guide, not a critical edition of every object

M. J. Geller's review in *The Historian* 82.2 (2020),
[DOI 10.1080/00182370.2020.1778940](https://doi.org/10.1080/00182370.2020.1778940),
identifies the catalog's distinctive combination of detailed object
descriptions on pp. 201–278 and thematic essays accessible to both specialists
and general readers. The review also calls the opening collecting history
somewhat nostalgic. That is a useful control: the catalog is an expert,
high-value interpretive gateway, but its institutional narrative and thematic
synthesis are not neutral object provenance or a substitute for specialist
editions and collation.

### Beckman 2023 and the long catalog series

Gary Beckman's 2023 JAOS review,
[DOI 10.7817/jaos.143.3.2023.br010](https://doi.org/10.7817/jaos.143.3.2023.br010),
independently stabilizes the Work's title, editors, New Haven publication,
2019 date, and physical-description conflict. Yale's official
[*Catalogue of the Babylonian Collections at Yale*](https://babylonian-collection.yale.edu/catalogue-babylonian-collections-yale)
and wider publication series demonstrate the correct next step for exact
objects: return from a general exhibition catalog to object-specific catalog
volumes, editions, hand copies, transliterations, translations, commentary,
and physical inspection where necessary.

For A02 this means the 2019 catalog may locate a school, literary, legal, or
wisdom object, but the exact object's current critical reading must come from
its dedicated publication history and witness evidence.

## 3. Fresh current relevance check

### The collection is active; the 2019 catalog is still a historical state

The current collection site reports an active mission to preserve, publish,
and make objects available. Its
[*Digitizing the Yale Babylonian Collection*](https://babylonian-collection.yale.edu/digitizing-yale-babylonian-collection-1)
page says the older FileMaker catalog was transferred into the Peabody system
in 2017, that cuneiform data are shared with CDLI and other projects, and that
large parts of the seals remain unpublished or need better documentation.
Klaus Wagensonner's current Yale profile dates the full digitization of about
35,000 cuneiform artifacts to 2019–2022 and says images are being exposed
through Peabody and other repositories.

The differing live counts — some 40,000 on the collection site, more than
45,000 on the Peabody page, about 35,000 cuneiform artifacts in the completed
digitization project — describe different dates and scopes. None is admitted
as exact Collection membership. A future reproducible inventory requires a
declared provider export, timestamp, query, identifiers, and fixity.

### 2026 portal transition changes the route, not the objects

The legacy Peabody collections portal announced retirement on 30 June 2026
and replacement by a new version of Yale LUX. The current Peabody collections
page states that all cataloged Peabody records are represented in
[LUX](https://lux.collections.yale.edu/), while the old collection site still
contains links to the legacy portal. This is current provider migration, not
object succession:

```text
legacy Peabody URL -> LUX representation
  != new physical object
  != unchanged record fields or stable URL
  != proof that every catalog assertion was reverified
```

ToS should prefer the literal museum catalog number plus recorded provider,
retrieval date, and cross-provider links. A LUX relationship is a discovery
projection and must not become a source-free graph assertion.

### 2024–2025 publications prove ongoing research, not blanket currency

The collection's current publication list includes *Late Babylonian
Administrative and Legal Texts, Concerning Craftsmen, from the Eanna Archive*
(2024) and *Middle Assyrian Texts, Seals, and Seal Impressions* (2025). These
volumes publish previously unpublished objects with descriptions, copies,
transliterations, translations, commentary, indexes, paleography, and, in the
2025 volume, pXRF provenience analysis. They show that Yale's holdings and
methods continue to produce new knowledge after the 2019 exhibition catalog.

They do not update Old Babylonian school, wisdom, law, or Gilgamesh objects by
default. Currency must be tested per object and textual tradition.
