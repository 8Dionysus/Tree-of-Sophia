# Exposure-aware solo+AI translation research

Snapshot: 2026-08-12.

Question: how can Tree of Sophia compare AI, AI+human, etymological,
semantic, and recognized-translation routes when the only human operator has
already lived with Zarathustra for five years, has also seen the exact
Antonovsky 2007 comparator page in the current project, and cannot make a
genuinely comparator-blind or German-competent human draft?

Status: ordered method research for a text-free experimental contract. This
packet contains no source text, candidate translation, comparator text,
accepted German, accepted Russian, alignment decision, or publication grant.

This report follows the required order: official and classical documentation,
then established high-impact work, then the freshest directly relevant
2025–2026 research. Freshness is recorded separately from authority.

## The premise that must be corrected

The frozen `translation-laboratory-plan.v1.json` was useful because it kept
source analysis, drafts, comparator reveal, change tracking, and adjudication
in order. Its global blindness assumption is not valid for this operator.

Dionysus has already reported five years of repeated reading, listening,
speaking aloud, and memorization of Zarathustra. That lived history is known at
Work level; its exact Expression, Item, dates, and retained wording are not.
Separately, the current project records a source-visible Workbench observation
of Antonovsky 2007 page 11. That observation is not attested as completed human
review, but it is sufficient evidence that the exact comparator Item and page
have already been displayed. Hiding the page in a later interface cannot erase
either exposure. Calling a later human draft `human_only` or comparator-blind
would make the provenance less true precisely where the golden kernel needs it
to be strongest.

The correction is additive. The old plan remains a frozen historical record.
A new exposure-aware layer governs future solo+AI experimental translation
episodes and states which old assumptions no longer apply.

## 1. Official and classical documentation

### ISO 17100 is a boundary, not a direct solo+AI recipe

- [ISO 17100:2015](https://www.iso.org/standard/59149.html?browse=ics) remains
  a published international standard, but its official lifecycle is
  `90.92 — to be revised` at this snapshot.
- The official scope states that raw machine-translation output plus
  post-editing is outside ISO 17100:2015. It therefore cannot be cited as if it
  directly certifies the ToS AI+human route.
- ToS adoption: preserve explicit process, responsibility, specifications,
  and review boundaries.
- ToS non-adoption: do not manufacture a professional multi-person service
  workflow inside a real solo+AI project and do not claim ISO conformity.

### MQM supplies a modular error vocabulary, not literary truth

- The [MQM Council overview](https://www.themqm.org/about-us/) says that the
  error typology is mostly stable while other MQM 2.0 components remain under
  development.
- The current [MQM typology](https://www.themqm.org/mqm-pillars/typology/)
  exposes seven high-level dimensions: terminology, accuracy, linguistic
  conventions, style, locale conventions, audience appropriateness, and
  design/markup. Implementers are expected to select a suitable granularity.
- MQM is applicable to human, machine, and post-edited translation, but its
  own guidance distinguishes analytic error annotation from holistic quality
  and recommends sampling rather than exhaustive review.
- ToS adoption: use a small typed error subset for actionable findings.
- ToS non-adoption: do not collapse rhythm, literary force, reader experience,
  source fidelity, and process provenance into one MQM score.

### WMT keeps human evaluation and document context visible

- The official [WMT 2025 General MT task](https://www2.statmt.org/wmt25/translation-task.html)
  used unseen documents, document-level literary material, human evaluation,
  automatic metrics, and an ESA-like source-aware protocol.
- Its submission law also requires system and data disclosure sufficient to
  distinguish methods.
- ToS adoption: freeze the exact document context and variant method before
  comparison; keep human and automatic evidence separate.
- ToS non-adoption: a shared-task rank, metric panel, or LLM judge cannot
  accept a philosophical translation.

### Translation-process evidence is distinct from product quality

- The [CRITT Translation Process Research Database](https://sites.google.com/site/centretranslationinnovation/tpr-db)
  separates raw user-activity data from post-processed tables and records
  keystrokes and gaze during translation and other text production.
- A current Translog-II method guide, [Translation Process Research in the
  Wild](https://www.mdpi.com/2306-5729/11/7/176), was published in 2026 and is
  useful for reproducible process capture.
- ToS adoption: preserve timestamps, candidate versions, edits, pauses when
  explicitly measured, and the exact exposure state under which an action
  occurred.
- ToS non-adoption: keystrokes, pauses, or edit distance do not reveal mental
  state and do not by themselves measure translation quality.

## 2. Established and high-impact work

### Retranslation cannot pretend the predecessor is absent

- Koskinen and Paloposki, 2015,
  [Anxieties of influence: The voice of the first translator in
  retranslation](https://doi.org/10.1075/target.27.1.01kos), studies how a
  prior translation remains an unavoidable function of retranslation and how
  retranslators adopt a stance toward it.
- ToS consequence: the Work-level lived history and the separately documented
  current-project Antonovsky exposure are evidence to preserve, not
  contamination to conceal. The human route is an exposure-aware
  retranslation or revision route, not an independent blind baseline.

### Post-editing changes both process and product

- Lacruz, Denkowski, and Lavie, 2014,
  [Cognitive demand and cognitive effort in
  post-editing](https://aclanthology.org/2014.amta-wptp.6/), shows why edit
  counts alone do not capture cognitive effort and separates transfer errors
  from mechanical errors.
- Castilho, Resende, and Mitkov, 2019,
  [What Influences the Features of
  Post-editese?](https://aclanthology.org/W19-8703/), reports evidence that
  post-edited output can retain characteristic traces, with effects varying by
  editing level, proficiency, and domain.
- Scarton et al., 2019,
  [Estimating post-editing effort](https://aclanthology.org/2019.iwslt-1.23/),
  finds task-based measurements more useful for the concrete post-editing task
  than detached reference metrics.
- ToS consequence: the AI+human result needs an intervention ledger and cannot
  be relabeled as an unaided human translation. Time and edit distance measure
  process cost, not literary or source quality.

### Controlled process corpora preserve actual interaction

- Sarti et al., 2022,
  [DivEMT](https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.532/), records edits,
  keystrokes, time, and pauses from professional translators working both from
  scratch and by post-editing. Productivity gains differ substantially across
  systems and languages.
- ToS consequence: cost, speed, and quality must remain separate axes, and the
  exact workflow is part of the experimental identity.

## 3. Fresh 2025–2026 research

### Adopt now as method pressure

- Zhang, Zhao, and Eger, NAACL 2025,
  [How Good Are LLMs for Literary Translation,
  Really?](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.548/), finds that evaluator
  expertise and rubric complexity materially change literary-translation
  judgments. In their setting a simpler best-worst scheme was more reliable
  than complex MQM annotation, and automatic metrics performed poorly.
  - Adoption: keep human forms short, criterion-specific, and competence
    scoped; use MQM only for targeted errors.
- Castaldo et al., MT Summit 2025,
  [Extending CREAMT](https://aclanthology.org/2025.mtsummit-1.40/), reports
  professional literary translators post-editing LLM output with measured
  time, quality, and creativity.
  - Adoption: preserve a distinct AI+human post-edit/retranslation lane and
    measure its real intervention cost.
- Wasti et al., ACL 2025,
  [TranslationCorrect](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-demo.53/), integrates
  post-editing with span-level error assistance and exports ESA-like evidence.
  - Adoption: future human work should expose source, candidate, targeted
    findings, and quick structured feedback in one episode; no retyping is
    required.
- Wu et al., TACL 2025,
  [TransAgents](https://aclanthology.org/2025.tacl-1.42/), separates preparation,
  translation, localization, proofreading, and final checking in a
  multi-agent literary workflow.
  - Adoption: role separation is useful, but simulated roles remain AI roles
    and do not create independent humans or additional competence.
- Zhang et al., Findings of ACL 2026,
  [Beyond Reproduction](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.2030/),
  evaluates source comprehension separately from creative potential and finds
  that strong comprehension does not imply human-level creativity.
  - Adoption: separate source-facing comprehension/fidelity evidence from
    target-language creativity and literary effect.
- Wen, Kang, and Sun, 2026,
  [Two audiences, two standards](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-026-07621-1),
  finds materially different judgments from bilingual and monolingual poetry
  readers: source access changes the operative evaluation standard.
  - Adoption: Dionysus may own Russian fluency, rhythm, voice, emotional
    resonance, and lived-reading judgments, but those judgments cannot be
    reported as German fidelity.

### Fresh evidence to retain with explicit limits

- Ferstler et al., June 2026 preprint,
  [AI translation of literary texts is "fine", but readers still prefer human
  translations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26040), separates immersive reading
  from close aligned comparison and reports that automatic and LLM judges do
  not recover reader preferences reliably.
  - Status: preprint, not final publication authority.
  - Adoption: use both uninterrupted reading and close comparison, and keep
    the two judgments separate.
- Scalena et al., EACL 2026,
  [Steering Large Language Models for Machine Translation
  Personalization](https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-long.217/), shows that
  translation style can be conditioned from examples and model
  representations.
  - Adoption: a model may inherit a translator's style from prompt examples or
    training. Absence of comparator text from a prompt proves only bounded
    context isolation, never absence from model weights.
- Griebel and Underwood, May 2026 preprint,
  [Fluency and Faithfulness in Human and Machine Literary
  Translation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15282), reports a fluency/faithfulness
  tension under automatic measurements over a large novel corpus.
  - Status: preprint and metric-dependent diagnostic evidence.
  - Adoption: never infer fidelity from fluent Russian and never fuse the two
    axes.
- [Assessing ChatGPT and Human Capabilities in Poetry
  Translation](https://journals.upress.ufl.edu/delos/article/view/3540), 2026,
  reports a preference shift after reviewers gained source access.
  - Adoption: record whether each judgment was target-only or source-visible.

## 4. Contract conclusions

### Blindness belongs to an actor and an event

One global comparator flag is insufficient. Every producer or reviewer needs
an exposure snapshot covering:

1. exact comparator text in the current prompt or visible interface;
2. comparator text supplied by retrieval, tools, or the current session;
3. other candidate or authored translation surfaces shown before freeze;
4. known historical human exposure and retained-memory risk;
5. unknown model-training exposure;
6. the time and actor of every later reveal.

`AI-only` may mean only that a run-specific model actor received no comparator
or other draft through its prompt, retrieval, tools, or session before its
candidate was frozen. It cannot mean that the model's training data is known
not to contain Antonovsky.

For Dionysus, withholding a fresh comparator redisplay can still reduce
immediate anchoring during candidate production. It does not undo the
five-year Work-level history or the exact current-project Antonovsky page
exposure, and it does not make the human historically blind. No retained
wording is inferred from either record.

### The four operative lanes

1. **Operationally isolated AI-only candidate.** Exact model, weights,
   runtime, prompt, decoding, source-analysis inputs, and exposure snapshot are
   frozen. Comparator and other drafts stay out of the run context. Model
   training exposure remains unknown.
2. **Exposure-aware AI+human retranslation.** Dionysus may judge and revise
   Russian voice, rhythm, pronounceability, clarity, intentional strangeness,
   literary force, and lived-reading fit. Known current-project Antonovsky
   exposure is disclosed; any influence claim remains self-reported, and every
   AI and human intervention remains attributable. This lane is not a blind
   baseline.
3. **Post-freeze comparator analysis.** A separately identified actor may see
   the exact recognized comparator only after the candidate is frozen. Its
   output is comparison evidence, not an independent candidate. Any later
   wording change enters a change ledger.
4. **Optional future independent human lane.** A genuinely comparator-naive,
   source-and-target-competent human would be a different reviewer. Under the
   current solo+AI operating model this is an external future condition, not a
   task or backlog.

### Evaluation remains partitioned

- **Source-facing:** German reading, morphology, syntax, primary and secondary
  meaning, omission, addition, metaphor, cultural allusion, etymology, and
  fidelity. AI findings remain cited proposals until competence-appropriate
  human review exists.
- **Target-facing:** Russian fluency, clarity, rhythm, voice, spoken
  pronounceability, intentional strangeness, literary force, and reader
  experience. Dionysus may make these judgments within the declared scope.
- **Process-facing:** elapsed and active time, model/runtime cost, edits,
  intervention attribution, abstentions, comparator reveal, and post-reveal
  changes. These measure workflow, not source truth.
- **Influence-facing:** wording remembered or recognized, convergence with the
  comparator, deliberate divergence, unresolved dependency, and reasons. No
  similarity score can prove where an influence came from.

### Human work stays rare and useful

No person should retype a page or repeat an already completed review merely to
exercise a UI. A human episode opens only after a candidate survives the
declared machine-only floor and a concrete translation question exists. The
ordinary episode offers source context, one or more frozen candidates, short
criteria, quick comments, targeted editing, and optional read-aloud evidence.
Keystroke or pause logging is optional and must not increase burden without a
specific process question.

Future A/B/C variants are frozen only after one exact translation question is
chosen. They compare candidate-producing methods; the recognized comparator
is a post-freeze reference, not a hidden fourth candidate or a quality oracle.
Quality, cost, and speed are reported independently.

## Stop line

This research corrects provenance and future method design. It does not
declare a translation good, independent, faithful, accepted, public, or ready
for semantic promotion.
