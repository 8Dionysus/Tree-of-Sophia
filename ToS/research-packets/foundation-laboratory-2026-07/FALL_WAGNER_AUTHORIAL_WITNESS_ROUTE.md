## Result

The exact 1888 Naumann Item now has a responsibility-aware upstream and
critical route:

```text
exact W II 6 / W II 7 regions only
  -> first print manuscript sent 26 June 1888
       -> returned 6 July as too difficult to place; lost
  -> new fair copy sent 16 July 1888; lost
       -> Nachschriften, Epilog, and proof-correction additions
            `-> surviving NL 200 : II, 1 correction/addition leaf
  -> author-published Naumann 1888 run of 1000 copies
       |-> unlabelled first half
       |    `-> existing MDZ/Bamberg Item
       `-> nominally labelled "Zweite Auflage" second half
            `-> no separately acquired ToS Item
  -> Colli/Montinari critical edition
       `-> intended eKGWB WA address family
```

This is not one linear immutable text. Nietzsche sent at least two complete
manuscript states, continued to add material while proofs were moving, and
only one correction/addition leaf is known to survive. That leaf records two
changes to the final printed pages; it is not the complete print manuscript.
The unlabelled and nominally labelled halves belong to the same 1000-copy
production run, but ToS does not infer either textual identity or textual
difference without a separately identified copy and comparison.

The authorial route also corrects a dangerous false mapping. `D 21`,
`GSA 71/28`, and ORES `75099` are the print manuscript of
*Götzen-Dämmerung*, not *Der Fall Wagner*. No `D`-number is assigned to the
lost full *Fall Wagner* print manuscripts by this research.

No new remote Item, manuscript image, source body, OCR, transcription,
critical-edition body, accepted reading, semantic annotation, translation
candidate, publication route, or recurring human queue is created.

## 2. Established production and publication route

Andreas Urs Sommer's 2012 HAdW
[overview](https://doi.org/10.11588/diglit.70913#0022) supplies the principal
production chronology:

- on 20 April 1888 Nietzsche announced a small pamphlet about music;
- on 26 June he sent the first print manuscript to Naumann and initially
  proposed Fraktur;
- on 28 June he revoked Fraktur in favour of Antiqua and sent additions;
- further additions followed on 1 July;
- on 6 July Naumann returned the full manuscript because placement of the
  additions was unclear;
- on 12 July Nietzsche acknowledged that even he found it difficult to read;
- on 16 July he sent a new fair copy and called it complete;
- on 2 August he sent material ending with what he called a Nachschrift;
- by 9 August proofs of the Nachschriften were expected, with Köselitz also
  correcting proofs;
- on 12 August Nietzsche sent the Epilog;
- printing was complete in September, and official publication followed on
  22 September 1888.

The complete first print manuscript and the 16 July fair copy are not known to
survive. The Basel leaf belongs to the later addition/correction phase; it
does not fill either loss.

Naumann produced 1000 copies. HAdW reports that the latter half was nominally
designated `Zweite Auflage`. The existing ToS Item is the exact
MDZ/Bamberg copy `30.1972`, object `bsb11827837`, B3Kat `BV020338451`.
Its visible title page does not carry `Zweite Auflage`. Its local generated
PDF remains a 75-page surrogate for 74 IIIF canvases with SHA-256
`37fc9eb2d26886be936efe06c7fbeaf9f6dab231b3a81bc2cb5e824e98f984ed`
and `NoC-NC 1.0` evidence.

The publication was still made under Nietzsche's supervision, unlike the
posthumous *Ecce homo* and *Antichrist* routes. That proximity does not turn
one digitized copy into an author-final abstraction or a critical edition.
Likewise, a nominal `Zweite Auflage` label does not by itself prove a second
textual state.

## 4. Established and fresh research

### Established topological control

HAdW's 2012 commentary is the historical-critical control for creation,
publication, the two lost complete manuscript states, the sole Basel leaf,
the 1000-copy run, and the nominal `Zweite Auflage`.

KGW IX 9, *Arbeitshefte W II 6 und W II 7* (ISBN
`978-3-11-022875-5`), is the established differentiated/topological
transcription. It preserves writing surface, sequence, revision, deletion,
and document state. A future ToS region comparison must retain the exact
page/layer address rather than extracting a smoothed fragment from the mixed
notebook.

### Fresh chronology and current object identity

Beat Röllin's open 2024 article
[*Chronologie der Manuskripte 1885–89. Nachtrag zu KGW IX*](https://doi.org/10.1515/nietzstu-2024-0001)
dates distinguishable inscription layers rather than whole notebooks. For the
current route it confirms multiple W II 6/W II 7 layers around spring,
June-July, late summer, and autumn 1888. This is the freshest direct
chronological control located.

The University of Basel's 11 April 2025 UNESCO notice and 2025 holdings
report identify `NL 200 : II, 1` as the *Der Fall Wagner* manuscript page and
the only surviving fragment. A later August 2025 exhibition notice confirms
that the Basel Nietzsche holdings are digitally available through
e-manuscripta. These are current identity and availability confirmations, not
new witness bodies.

Paolo D'Iorio's 2024 article, published online in January 2025
([DOI 10.1590/2316-82422024v4503pd](https://doi.org/10.1590/2316-82422024v4503pd)),
is retained as a current Nietzsche Source method and documentation control,
not as a manuscript witness.

### Fresh interpretive and reuse leads kept downstream

Three current leads are useful but do not change identity:

- Barbara Neymeyr's 2025 open article
  ([DOI 10.1111/glal.70004](https://doi.org/10.1111/glal.70004))
  treats Bülow's critique of Nietzsche's composition as a possible background
  to the later Wagner polemics. It does not establish the direct Bülow message
  that HAdW treats as an archive-mediated legend.
- Musil Online's 1 September 2025 release supplies a CC BY-SA 4.0 TEI surface
  for an 1899 sixth/seventh-thousand edition. That can become a future textual
  challenger, but it is not the 1888 Item, an authorial manuscript, or the
  critical text.
- Markus Wölfl's chapter *Zum „unsichtbaren Theater“. Nietzsche und der Fall
  Wagner* ([DOI 10.30965/9783846770979-009](https://doi.org/10.30965/9783846770979-009))
  is publisher-dated 10 June 2026. Only metadata was located; no abstract or
  full text was available. Its direct title makes it relevant, but not a basis
  for rewriting the witness route.

General-web results were checked last. Modern reprints, commercial
audiobooks, blogs, and generated summaries frequently collapse manuscript,
first print, modern text, and interpretation. They were rejected as identity
or critical authority.
