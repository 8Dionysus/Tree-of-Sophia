## Result first

The exact 1899 Edition is now both institutionally identified and locally
source-returnable. RuNEB exposes an official ZIP containing three intact PDF
parts from the Russian National Library object `ob000000411`. The 530 MB
archive and all members passed fixity checks. The title page confirms the
Moscow 1899 *Reader* edition, Russian translation from German, issuing
editorial office, and a separate distribution statement.

The digital object is not a complete book. It contains 22 photographed
surfaces: the title page and selected spreads through printed pages 138–139.
The RuNEB card catalogs 236 pages. ToS therefore creates a verified Edition
and a verified **fragmentary digital Item**, not a complete source, accepted
text, or collated expression.

The title page does not name a translator. A contemporary 1901 report says the
separate book was reprinted from the *New Journal* without Antonovsky's
permission or name, but the surviving fragment has not been collated against
the 1898 journal state. ToS creates a provisional uncredited Russian
Expression and explicitly withholds a `translated_by` claim and every
1898/1899/1900 derivation or equivalence edge.

### 1.2 Exact source pages

Direct inspection of the fixity-bound first PDF surface shows the historical
title `Такъ говорилъ Заратустра`, the printed German parenthetical `(Also
sprach Sarathustra)`, `Переводъ съ нѣмецкаго`, and the issuing statement
`Изданіе редакціи журнала „Читатель“`. It separately prints the distribution
line `Складъ изд. у Д. П. Ефимова, Москва, Тверская, д. Мухина, кв. 17` and
the year 1899.

The publisher and distributor are therefore represented as two provision
activities. D. P. Efimov remains an unnormalized literal agent; the title page
does not establish that person as publisher or printer.

## 2. Established and direction-defining evidence

The 1901 Nevedomsky essay in the official KazNEB copy of Lichtenberger's
*Philosophy of Nietzsche* reports that the separately issued book reprinted
material from the *New Journal* without Antonovsky's permission and without
naming him. Its later citation to page 89 of the *Reader* edition joins the
report to this exact bibliographic route.

Antonovsky's source-visible 1911 preface separately calls the 1898 appearances
excerpts and counts his edition sequence as 1900, 1903, 1907, and 1911. These
statements explain why 1898 first appearance, uncredited 1899 book, and first
Antonovsky-counted 1900 edition can coexist.

Neither report is a line-by-line collation. The source fragment now makes a
future comparison possible, but it does not justify an Antonovsky
responsibility claim, a complete 1899 text, or an edge from 1899 to 1898 or
1900.
