## Outcome first

The backlog label names a useful research domain, but not one provider-independent object with a closed membership set. Nippur and Ur yielded different, historically formed assemblages. Their surviving exercise, lexical, literary, mathematical, legal, administrative, and other tablets were excavated, recycled, dispersed, cataloged, and published through different routes. Treating the phrase as a literal Collection would make an interpretive grouping look like ancient or custodial fact.

The narrow source witness is therefore the modern article Work:

`tos.work.old-babylonian-scholarship.texts-tablets-and-teaching`

Steve Tinney's *Texts, Tablets, and Teaching: Scribal Education in Nippur and Ur* is the best compact exact bridge between both sites, tablet forms, curricular stages, archaeological context, and preservation bias. It is a source route, not the array itself. Every future tablet still requires its own artifact identity, provider and holding record, provenience, context, classification, representation, text, rights, and review.

## 1. Official and classical documentation

### Penn Museum: exact cross-site source Work

The official Penn Museum article [*Texts, Tablets, and Teaching*](https://www.penn.museum/sites/expedition/texts-tablets-and-teaching/) is subtitled “Scribal Education in Nippur and Ur”, credited to Steve Tinney, and identified by the museum as originally published in 1998. Its citation gives *Expedition* 40(2), July 1998. The current [Penn contributor page](https://www.penn.museum/people/person/102) uses the fuller form Stephen J. Tinney and lists the same article, volume, issue, and date.

The article supplies the exact controls needed by A02:

- Ur's lexical and literary finds become informative through comparison with the much larger Nippur body, but the two sites are not interchangeable.
- Wedge practice, Syllable Alphabet B, Tu-ta-ti, name lists, word lists, advanced lexical series, literary texts, and isolated extracts describe a sequence of different activities rather than one undifferentiated “content” layer.
- Type II tablets can preserve a teacher's exemplar and an erasable student area; lentils, single-column extracts, and multi-column texts preserve different material arrangements.
- Type II predominance at Nippur and near-absence at Ur may reflect recyclability, historical events, and archaeological accident, not a simple curricular absence.
- Literary work was added to lexical work rather than necessarily replacing it.
- Small extracts cannot always be ordered into a demonstrated sequence.
- Student exercises are not automatically superior or authoritative copies of literary Works.
- Private-house contexts and the more than two thousand texts from Ur's “No. 7 Quiet Street” show why object and context evidence must accompany textual classification.
- Two differently spelled copies may be compatible with dictation, but Tinney marks that inference as unprovable.

The page also warns that the digitized article is a historical reference and may not reflect current Penn Museum views. That warning is preserved, not hidden by the verified Work identity.

### ORACC OBMC: exact queryable subset, not the whole array

The official ORACC [Old Babylonian School](https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/obmc/OldBabylonianSchool/index.html) overview, authored by Gabriella Spada in 2019, presents abundant Old Babylonian teaching evidence across several cities and points to Nippur as the principal basis for reconstructing an early curriculum. Its live [P231446 route](https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/obmc/P231446) identifies `Ist Ni 01576` as a Nippur school Type II tablet: the obverse carries a model contract and the reverse `OB Nippur Ura 6`. The current provider facets report 294 Nippur and 6 Ur records inside this Old Babylonian Model Contracts surface.

Those counts are a dated live observation of one legal/model-contract project, not the size of all school-tablet assemblages. ORACC supplies exact machine-queryable candidates and tablet-type facets; it does not convert a legal-training subset into the identity of the lexical and literary array.

This is a strong institutional route for exact objects held at Yale. It is not evidence that every Yale school-looking tablet came from Nippur or Ur, nor that collection membership proves ancient educational use.

### Classical Nippur and Ur publication line

- Hermann V. Hilprecht, [*Explorations in Bible Lands during the 19th Century*](https://commons.library.stonybrook.edu/amar/463/) (1903), gives the classical “temple library and school” interpretation and an early description of clumsy Old Babylonian exercises. Its historical claim is evidence in the history of classification, not final archaeological fact. The Stony Brook digitization states noncommercial reuse restrictions; no 83.5 MB scan was acquired.
- Edward Chiera, [*Sumerian Lexical Texts from the Temple School of Nippur*](https://isac.uchicago.edu/research/publications/oip/oip-11-cuneiform-series-volume-i-sumerian-lexical-texts-temple-school) (OIP 11, 1929), publishes 126 plates of lexical fragments from Nippur preserved in the Philadelphia Museum. “Temple School” is retained as the historical publication title, not promoted as an uncontested institution classification.
- The British Institute for the Study of Iraq's current [Ur publication guide](https://www.bisi.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/Ur.pdf) identifies C. J. Gadd and S. N. Kramer's *Ur Excavations Texts VI: Literary and Religious Texts*, parts 1 and 2 (1963, 1966), and Kramer and Shaffer's third part (2006). These are publication routes to Ur witnesses, not one current corpus release or automatic rights grant.

## 2. Established and field-shaping scholarship

- Niek Veldhuis, [*Elementary Education at Nippur: The Lists of Trees and Wooden Objects*](https://research.rug.nl/en/publications/elementary-education-at-nippur-the-lists-of-trees-and-wooden-obje/) (University of Groningen dissertation, 1997), provides the detailed elementary-sequence control used by Tinney. The institutional record exposes the thesis in parts but states that distribution is not generally permitted absent an applicable open-content route. No payload was acquired.
- Steve Tinney, [“On the Curricular Setting of Sumerian Literature”](https://doi.org/10.2307/4200472), *Iraq* 61 (1999): 159–172, is the field-shaping reconstruction of the Nippur literary curriculum. It remains a local, source-bounded reconstruction, not a universal Old Babylonian rule.
- Eleanor Robson, [“The Tablet House: A Scribal School in Old Babylonian Nippur”](https://doi.org/10.3917/assy.093.0039), *Revue d'assyriologie* 93 (2001): 39–66, supplies the House F archaeological control and prevents literary school representations from substituting for assemblage and building evidence.
- Paul Delnero, [“Sumerian Extract Tablets and Scribal Education”](https://doi.org/10.1086/JCS41103871), *Journal of Cuneiform Studies* 62(1) (2010): 53–69, is the direct established control for interpreting extract tablets and their educational role without treating format as self-explanatory.
- Dominique Charpin, [*Le Clergé d'Ur au siècle d'Hammurabi*](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb34874721m) (Droz, 1986), supplies the established Ur residential and institutional context behind “No. 7 Quiet Street”. The BnF record identifies a restricted print book; no content was acquired.

Together these works require locality, material form, excavation context, formation process, textual function, and scholarly reconstruction to remain separate.

The volume covers archaeology, elementary sequence, complex lists, bilingualism, legal practice, the Decad, errors, literature, and catalog concordances. It is a current object-returnable control, not a replacement for exact artifact records.

## Why no Collection is created

An exact ToS Collection would require at minimum:

1. a declared membership rule;
2. a bounded member list;
3. provider-independent artifact reconciliation;
4. provenience and holding evidence per object;
5. classification and uncertainty claims per object;
6. versioned additions, removals, and disputed members;
7. rights posture for every representation and text layer.

The current phrase provides none of those as one authoritative set. A research-defined virtual Collection may become useful later, but only after its construction is explicit. Creating it now would turn the research question into false source fact.
