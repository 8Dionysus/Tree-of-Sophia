Date of evidence cut: 2026-08-23

Status: research completed for a metadata-only control-source planting. No

## Exact backlog pressure

This packet addresses line 21 of the A02 branch-local source backlog:
`Niek Veldhuis, Elementary Education at Nippur`, whose compact contribution is
`ранние ступени curriculum, lexical sequence`.

The source is foundational because it turns surviving exercise tablets into a
reconstructable sequence. That strength is also the main risk. A modern
sequence inferred from tablet form, the relation between obverse and reverse,
repeated list combinations, and local frequency is not automatically an
ancient syllabus, a school-board rule, one institution, an individual pupil's
complete biography, or a universal Mesopotamian curriculum.

The bounded source planting therefore separates:

- the 1997 scholarly Work and its bibliographic identity;
- the physical exercise tablets and their archaeological contexts;
- modern tablet-type labels such as `Type II` or `teacher-student exercise`;
- source-visible pairings between one tablet's obverse and reverse;
- the reconstructed local order called the `Nippur curriculum`;
- cross-site comparison and any claim of broader uniformity;
- later critical reframing of what `curriculum`, `school`, and
  `classification` mean.

## 2. Established and field-shaping scholarship

Veldhuis's 1997 thesis is itself the detailed field-shaping source for the
elementary sequence, especially the lists of trees and wooden objects and the
use of paired teacher-student exercise tablets.

The existing A02 research route keeps it in relation to:

- Steve Tinney, `On the Curricular Setting of Sumerian Literature` (1999),
  which moves from elementary lexical education toward the literary phase but
  remains a local reconstruction;
- Eleanor Robson, `The Tablet House` (2001), which restores House F's
  architecture, assemblage, deposition, and tablet typology;
- Paul Delnero, `Sumerian Extract Tablets and Scribal Education` (2010), which
  prevents extract format from becoming a self-evident pedagogical identity;
- Niek Veldhuis, *History of the Cuneiform Lexical Tradition* (2014), which
  places one Old Babylonian local sequence inside a much longer, changing
  tradition of lexical scholarship.

The established inference is strong but conditional: repeated tablet pairings
and exercise forms can support a local relative sequence. They do not erase
variation, missing tablets, secondary deposition, teacher choice, changing
practice, or the distinction between reconstructed order and ancient
administration.

## 3. Fresh current relevance check

### Veldhuis's 2023 reconstruction

The newest direct self-return found is Veldhuis's chapter `Reconstructing the
Elementary Nippur Curriculum` in the official ISAC volume *Back to School in
Babylonia* (2023):

- <https://isac.uchicago.edu/research/publications/ISACMP/isacmp1>

The chapter is not merely a reprint of the 1997 thesis. It gives ToS several
current controls:

- more than 3,000 exercises are reported from Nippur, spanning early scribbles
  through advanced lists, proverbs, and model contracts;
- `teacher-student` tablets preserve an exemplar and a pupil copy, while the
  relation between the current exercise and an older reverse exercise helps
  reconstruct relative progression;
- the evidence supports an unusually detailed local Nippur sequence;
- other centers differ: for example, lists of divine names occur at different
  stages outside Nippur;
- the word `curriculum` may misleadingly suggest a board or administrative
  authority enforcing one sequence; the chapter explicitly rejects that
  model for Babylonia and instead places teaching with private practitioners;
- relative cross-Babylonian uniformity therefore remains a historical pattern
  requiring explanation, not proof of one centralized institution.

This is the current semantic correction ToS must preserve: `curriculum` is a
useful scholarly label for a reconstructed pattern, not an ancient actor or a
self-identifying institution.

### 2026 relevance without calendar inflation

UCL Press published the open-access volume *Scribal Worlds: Scholarship and
classification in cuneiform cultures* on 29 June 2026:

- <https://uclpress.co.uk/book/scribal-worlds/>
- DOI <https://doi.org/10.14324/111.9781806550821>

The volume is inspired by Veldhuis's work and explicitly joins language,
ontology, classification, scribal learning, material culture, and philosophy
of science. It is highly relevant to ToS's semantic direction because it
treats classification as historically and materially situated. It does not,
on the checked publisher evidence, supersede the exact 1997/2023
reconstruction of the elementary Nippur sequence. It remains a current
contextual control, not a replacement identity or automatic update.

No directly relevant 2024-2026 publication was found that overturns the
bounded local sequence or restores a centralized universal curriculum model.
Calendar-new cuneiform OCR, sign detection, and transliteration datasets may
become laboratory challengers for tablet readings; they do not establish the
historical order, institution, or pedagogical meaning of the Nippur evidence.

1. Create one modern scholarly Work for the 1997 thesis, not an ancient Work,
   curriculum entity, school organization, Collection, or tablet family.
2. Create one provisional Agent coordinating the checked `Niek Veldhuis` and
   `Nicolaas Christiaan Veldhuis` forms, without external equivalence claims.

   local object evidence, not as an immutable ancient syllabus.
4. Keep modern tablet types, physical artifacts, obverse/reverse relations,
   exercise contents, relative sequence, and cross-site comparison in
   separate layers.
5. Preserve the 2023 chapter as a distinct contained Work in an aggregate

6. Preserve *Scribal Worlds* 2026 as the newest relevant classification and
   contextualization control, not as direct supersession of the exact

## Rights posture

The University of Groningen page makes the thesis available in parts and as a
complete file but does not, on the checked record, establish a reusable
work-level open license. The portal names University of Groningen rights and
reserves site content subject to applicable open-content terms. Availability
therefore supports discovery and local lawful research, not automatic ToS
redistribution.

The 2023 ISAC volume remains governed by its one-copy personal-use and
official-site distribution conditions already recorded in A02. The 2026 UCL
Press volume declares an open-access route and a CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 content
posture in the existing discovery record, but this planting stores neither
volume. Every future chapter, image, transcription, translation, and derived
dataset requires its own layer-specific rights and provenance decision.

tablet type, or machine-detected sign cannot by itself verify historical

meaning.
