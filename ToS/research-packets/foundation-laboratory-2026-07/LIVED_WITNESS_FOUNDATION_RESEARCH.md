# Lived-witness foundation research

Status: ordered method and contract research; no testimony captured, inferred,
scheduled, indexed, graphed, published, or promoted

Research snapshot: 2026-08-08

Owner route: `ToS/research-packets/AGENTS.md`

## Result first

Tree of Sophia needs a dedicated **first-person authored record**, not a new
semantic annotation type and not a renamed source witness.

The route should preserve what a human author says about living with a work,
when and in what context they choose to say it, which Work or passage it
addresses, how the record was captured or transformed, and which later uses
are separately permitted. It should begin private and local. AI may ask,
transcribe, organize metadata, or prepare a visibly separate derivative, but
AI output alone cannot possess lived experience or silently enter the
testimony body.

This creates a durable place for the five-year lived route through
*Zarathustra* without converting love, war, death, suffering, joy, memory, or
interpretation into a machine ontology. It also does not create another
recurring human-review queue: capture opens only on an explicit author request.

## Exact pressure in the current foundation

ToS doctrine already names `lived_witness` as an assertion layer and permits it
to explain attention, salience, and continuity. The generic claim schema also
names it. Before this slice, however, the repository had no dedicated route
for:

- exact first-person authorship and author confirmation;
- experience time distinct from recording time;
- work-level, passage-level, event-level, and life-context targets;
- capture mode, raw artifact, AI role, and visible transformation lineage;
- third-party exposure and redaction review;
- separate permissions for local storage, Git metadata, quotation, lexical or
  semantic indexing, graph projection, publication, model training, and
  external processing;
- revision, supersession, and withdrawal;
- author-retained copyright distinct from use permissions;
- a private-by-default storage path and a conversational human form.

The gap matters because a generic claim packet can label a claim
`lived_witness` while still failing to preserve the authored body, capture
conditions, relational privacy, or permission lifecycle.

## I. Classical standards and official guidance

### W3C Web Annotation Data Model

The stable W3C Recommendation models an annotation as a body related to one or
more targets. It also provides lifecycle, agent, motivation, rights, selector,
and source-state structures:

<https://www.w3.org/TR/annotation-model/>

The useful ToS profile is narrow:

- one authored body may address the whole Work or several exact targets;
- passage targets may use ToS source-returnable anchors and selectors;
- a whole-work or life-context testimony must not be forced into a fake quote
  selector;
- body and target remain distinct, so first-person testimony does not become
  Nietzsche's text;
- private annotation migration is a valid use case, so interoperability does
  not imply publication.

ToS does not claim Web Annotation conformance for the packet. It adopts the
body/target/lifecycle separation while retaining ToS IDs and authority law.

### W3C PROV-O

PROV-O separates Entity, Activity, and Agent, and provides attribution,
association, generation time, derivation, and revision relations:

<https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/>

For ToS this means:

- the human-authored record is an Entity attributed to the human Agent;
- capture, transcription, author confirmation, revision, and visibility change
  are separate Activities;
- a later summary or interpretation is derived from the record rather than
  silently replacing it;
- a revision remains connected to the prior version.

PROV-O is used as a conceptual mapping through the existing ToS provenance
event contract; no RDF store is required.

### Oral History Association principles and best practices

OHA guidance treats preparation, interviewing, preservation, and access as
connected from the start. It calls for documented informed consent, an
opportunity for narrator approval before public release where possible,
explicit plans for recording/review/preservation/access/use, respect for
refusal and access restrictions, contextual metadata, and integrity of the
narrator's perspective:

<https://oralhistory.org/principles-and-best-practices-revised-2018/>

<https://oralhistory.org/best-practices/>

<https://oralhistory.org/for-participants-in-oral-history-interviews/>

The ToS route is not represented as a formal oral-history project. These are
ethical analogues for author sovereignty, ongoing participation, review before
release, preservation, contextualization, restriction, and honest withdrawal
limits. The solo author is not required to sign a performative institutional
release to store their own private record.

### TEI critical-apparatus witness is the wrong identity

TEI P5 `att.witnessed` identifies documentary witnesses supporting a reading
in a critical apparatus:

<https://tei-c.org/release/doc/tei-p5-doc/en/html/ref-att.witnessed.html>

That meaning must not be reused for lived testimony. A manuscript, print, or
edition witness attests a textual reading; a human lived witness reports a
first-person experience. Collapsing the two would weaken both text criticism
and personal authorship.

## II. Established and direction-defining work

### Personal annotation is a path of interpretation

John Bradley distinguishes richer scholarly/personal annotation practices from
simple web tagging and shows how annotations participate in developing an
interpretation:

<https://dhq.digitalhumanities.org/vol/6/2/000121/000121.html>

This supports keeping a lived record addressable and revisable without
pretending that it is a source property or universally shared reading.

The audiated-annotation work is especially relevant to a book repeatedly
spoken and heard: it treats listening and voiced reading as a meaningful
scholarly interaction rather than a lossy copy of silent text:

<https://www.digitalhumanities.org/dhq/vol/15/1/000512/000512.html>

It supports `listened-to`, `recited`, and voice-recording modes, but does not
authorize ToS to infer what the author's five years meant.

### Autoethnographic evidence requires relational ethics

Jane Edwards' review of ethical autoethnography emphasizes that using the self
as process and product still implicates relationships and other people:

<https://doi.org/10.1177/1609406921995306>

Martin Tolich's guidelines argue that autoethnographic ethics must be
anticipated rather than repaired only after publication:

<https://doi.org/10.1177/1049732310376076>

The practical ToS consequence is a third-party posture and a publication block
until exposure/redaction is reviewed. The original local body is not silently
redacted; any redacted publication candidate is a separate derivative.

### Memory has value and limits as evidence

Oelofse and de Bruyn examine memory as oral-history evidence and the need to
hold both its value and its limitations:

<https://doi.org/10.38140/sjch.v30i2.466>

ToS therefore records lived witness as first-person recollection. It is
authoritative for the authored report, not automatic objective proof of every
external event described.

## III. Freshest relevant work checked on 2026-08-08

### Context and consent under analytical and generative AI

Mary Larson's 2026 review centers context, consent, datafication, analytical
AI, and generative AI in oral-history practice:

<https://doi.org/10.1080/00940798.2026.2625663>

This reinforces separate permission for analysis and generation rather than a
single broad `AI use` switch.

### LLM output lacks testimonial grounding

Donner's May 2026 document-theory analysis argues that LLM outputs lack
identifiable authorship, stable provenance, and testimonial grounding unless a
situated human process gives them documentary force:

<https://doi.org/10.1002/asi.70090>

The ToS consequence is categorical: an LLM-generated first-person narrative is
not lived witness. Human approval may establish authorship of an explicitly
AI-assisted edited text, but the AI role and transformations must remain
visible.

The June 2026 study of synthetic lived-experience language shows the same risk
in direct form: peer-like AI may imply experiences it cannot possess:

<https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18057>

### AI can alter recall and narrative structure

The CHI 2026 study of GPT-assisted recall examines how interaction with an LLM
can affect accounts of witnessed events:

<https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791863>

The July 2026 oral-history visualization study reports conflicts between scene
planning and preservation of source testimony:

<https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24756>

These results argue for preserving the first raw answer, model/prompt lineage,
and every transformation before using AI to polish, summarize, visualize, or
organize a narrative.

### Human interpretive control and traceability remain necessary

The January 2026 human-AI thematic-analysis protocol keeps modification,
rejection, insertion, and commentary with the human analyst and emphasizes
trace-to-text auditability:

<https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2601.11850>

The 2026 NLP4DH proceedings add a directly adjacent provenance result: OCR
correction pathways can change downstream interpretation, so corrections and
revision state should remain first-class rather than overwrite one another:

<https://aclanthology.org/volumes/2026.nlp4dh-1/>

Although these works concern thematic analysis and text correction rather than
one personal record, the transferable method is the same: preserve source
state, lineage, human intervention, and rejection paths.

### Ongoing consent and changing access decisions

The May 2026 hybrid-epistemology study of an interview archive reports an
ongoing-consent practice in which narrators could reaffirm or change access and
identification decisions before public release:

<https://doi.org/10.1177/16094069261456042>

This supports versioned permissions and withdrawal rather than one irreversible
blanket consent. It also supports separating authorship from anonymity and
public identification choices.

### Freshness conclusion

The refresh searched current official pages, DOI-indexed 2026 work, ACL
Anthology, and recent arXiv records through 2026-08-08. No later primary result
found in that pass supports replacing first-person authorship with generated
narrative, merging all permissions, or letting model-produced interpretation
become testimony. The newest relevant results strengthen raw preservation,
context, explicit AI role, human confirmation, and separate downstream-use
gates.

## ToS profile decision

| Question | Decision | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What is lived witness? | a first-person human-authored record | AI and documentary witnesses cannot possess the author's lived route |
| Where does the exact body live? | ignored `ToS/zarathustra/lived-witness/local-content/` | privacy and author control precede interoperability |
| What is tracked now? | schema, route, protocol, blank conversational form, research, and structural checks | no personal content or implied testimony is needed to prepare the route |
| What time is recorded? | experience scope and capture time separately | recollection time is not record-generation time |
| Must every record cite a passage? | no; Work target is sufficient when truthful | forced anchors manufacture precision |
| Can a record cite passages? | yes, through source-returnable ToS anchors | exact textual relevance should remain recoverable |
| May AI help? | questions, transcription, metadata, visible edit proposals, separate derivatives | assistance is useful when provenance and author confirmation remain explicit |
| May AI write lived witness? | no | generated first-person language lacks lived experience and testimonial grounding |
| How are edits handled? | preserve raw input; list transformations; confirm exact final digest | no silent normalization or narrative polishing |
| How are other people protected? | third-party risk review before any derivative leaves local storage | personal testimony can expose relational subjects |
| Is there one consent switch? | no; nine purpose-specific decisions | storage, quotation, indexes, graph, release, training, and external processing are materially different |
| What is the default? | local storage granted; every other use not granted | preparation must not create publication or training authority |
| Who owns the words? | the human author retains copyright by default | capture and confirmation are not a license or assignment |
| Can it become semantics or canon? | only through a separate claim and human review | testimony may guide interpretation but cannot settle it |
| Is capture scheduled? | no; explicit author invocation only | the route must serve the human, not create clerical debt |

## Rejected alternatives

- **Reuse TEI `wit`: rejected.** It would conflate a critical-text witness with
  a person reporting lived experience.
- **Store testimony directly in generic `claim-packet`: rejected as the primary
  record.** The generic shape lacks capture, temporal, relational-privacy,
  permission, and exact-body confirmation detail.
- **Track a metadata receipt automatically: rejected.** Even the existence,
  date, or context of a private record may be sensitive; tracked metadata
  requires its own permission.
- **One broad consent or open license: rejected.** It cannot express different
  risk and intent for quotation, indexing, graph projection, public release,
  model training, and external processing.
- **AI-polished first-person narrative as the default: rejected.** It risks
  synthetic experience, recall influence, and loss of the author's idiom.
- **Mandatory passage anchoring: rejected.** A five-year relation to the whole
  work or a life interval may be the truthful scope.
- **Recurring human review queue: rejected.** Lived testimony is a voluntary
  author event, not throughput work.
- **In-place tightening of the generic `claim-packet` schema: rejected in this
  slice.** Existing source-witness provenance events bind the exact digest of
  that v1 schema. Mutating it would invalidate historical fixity, while
  rewriting old event digests would falsely claim they ran under the new
  contract. Human-only authorship is enforced by the dedicated lived-witness
  packet and route now; a future generic claim change requires a versioned
  schema and explicit migration.

## Implemented contract boundary

The research admits:

- `ToS/contracts/lived-witness-packet.schema.json`;
- `lived_witness_capture`, `authorship_confirmation`, and
  `visibility_change` provenance event kinds;
- the private route, conversational form, narrow ignore rule, and structural
  validation.

It does **not** admit:

- a personal testimony body;
- a tracked metadata envelope;
- a semantic/sign/concept/relation candidate;
- a graph or retrieval projection;
- model training data;
- public release;
- a new Human Gold task.

## Manual reality check required when first used

When the author activates the route, structural validation is insufficient.
The human must directly verify:

1. the exact displayed body is what they intend to say;
2. every AI or editorial transformation is visible;
3. experience time and context are stated with the intended precision;
4. targets and passage anchors reflect the record rather than force it;
5. third-party exposure is acceptable for the chosen use;
6. each non-local permission is separately and deliberately granted;
7. the record remains outside source, philology, semantic truth, and canon.

Until that event occurs, the honest state is **route prepared; lived witness
not captured**.
