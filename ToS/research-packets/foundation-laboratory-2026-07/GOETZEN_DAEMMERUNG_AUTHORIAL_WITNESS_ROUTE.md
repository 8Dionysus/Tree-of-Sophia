## 2. Established production and publication route

Andreas Urs Sommer's 2012 HAdW
[commentary](https://doi.org/10.11588/diglit.70913) supplies the principal
production and text-history control:

- the book grew through continuous selection, revision, reuse, and
  recontextualisation of 1887-1888 revaluation material;
- an early combined project also contained material later becoming
  *Der Antichrist* 1-24, so the two published works share preparatory soil
  without becoming one immutable work;
- Nietzsche decided in early September 1888 to print
  *Müssiggang eines Psychologen* while preparing a new
  *Umwerthung aller Werthe*;
- he sent the clean print manuscript to Naumann on 7 September;
- *Was den Deutschen abgeht* followed on 18 September;
- Heinrich Köselitz suggested changing the title, and Nietzsche adopted
  *Götzen-Dämmerung* by 27 September. Suggestion and authorial adoption are
  separate responsibility events, not joint authorship;
- *Streifzüge eines Unzeitgemässen* §§32-44 were added during correction in
  the first half of October;
- *Was ich den Alten verdanke* migrated late in October from an
  `Ur-Ecce-homo` state addressed in W II 9c;
- printing was complete on 13 November;
- Nietzsche received four finished copies on 24 November and arranged
  advance copies;
- the title page bears 1889, but production and authorial receipt belong to
  November 1888;
- sale was deliberately held and public distribution began at the end of
  January 1889, commonly dated 24 January.

The exact existing MDZ/BSB Item is the Bayerische Staatsbibliothek copy
`Ph.pr. 881 kc`, MDZ `bsb00069119`, B3Kat `BV020368110`, URN
`urn:nbn:de:bvb:12-bsb00069119-0`, OCLC `21010699`. Its local generated PDF
has 165 pages for 164 source canvases plus one provider cover, SHA-256
`f41a1dee091edd895d1f18a510dc73b48949257e882d3390c6c8f72beeb8d086`,
and concrete MDZ `CC BY-NC-SA 4.0` evidence. The visible copy has no edition
statement. It remains the exact first-publication Item already in ToS, not an
abstract author-final or critical text.

### The 1893 second edition is a separate editorial state

The 1893 second edition arranged by Heinrich Köselitz added headings and
introduced textual changes that later critical editing removed. Nietzsche
did not approve that edition. ToS therefore must not use an “1893” label as
an interchangeable copy of the existing Item, or infer the second edition
from a modern catalog's loose edition numbering.

No exact 1893 Item is acquired or created by this route.

## 4. Established and fresh research

### A fresh reconstruction remains a challenger
