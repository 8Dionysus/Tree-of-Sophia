# A02 Old Babylonian Sumerian literary catalogues: source-planting research

Date of evidence cut: 2026-08-23

Status: research completed for a metadata-only source planting. No catalogue
transliteration, translation, tablet image, line art, scan, facsimile, or
publication body is admitted by this packet.

## Exact backlog pressure

This packet addresses line 18 of the A02 branch-local source backlog:
`curricular catalogues of Sumerian literature`, described there as a
school-library instrument contributing order and grouping to the educational
corpus.

The backlog wording is useful as a research lead but is too strong as an
identity claim. The surviving objects do not by themselves constitute one
ancient Work, one complete bibliography, one universal curriculum, one canon,
or one stable ordering of Sumerian literature.

The bounded result separates:

- each physical catalogue tablet;
- each tablet's local ordered sequence of incipits and ruled sections;
- the modern identification of an incipit with one or more compositions;
- a provider-independent scholarly composite coordinating the catalogue
  witnesses without claiming that they are copies of one ancient original;
- bounded evidence for the Decad as a fixed group and sequence;
- broader curriculum, library, canon, corpus-completeness, and semantic claims.

## 1. Official and classical documentation

### Ancient catalogue coverage

The Oxford ETCSL ancient-catalogues page distinguishes two Ur III literary
catalogues from eleven Isin-Larsa/Old Babylonian entries: N2, L, U1, U2, U3,
N3, B1, N4, B4, Y2, and N6:

- <https://etcsl.orinst.ox.ac.uk/catalogue/catalogue0.htm>

This is a useful historical index, not a current complete membership list. The
page was created in 1998 and last revised in 2002. Its labels and composition
links remain provider representations and modern editorial mappings.

The distinct complete ETCSL catalogue, updated in 2006 and retained by Oxford
Text Archive, names thirteen Old Babylonian entries. It adds 0.2.17, an Old
Babylonian letter catalogue from Uruk (W 17259 an), and 0.2.18, an Old
Babylonian catalogue from Sippar (Si 331):

- <https://ota.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/repository/xmlui/bitstream/handle/20.500.12024/2518/etcslfullcat.html?isAllowed=y&sequence=7>

The eleven-versus-thirteen difference is preserved as provider-surface and
revision coverage, not repaired by silently choosing the larger number. It
proves that neither ETCSL page can stand for a closed ancient witness set and
that coverage must retain the exact surface, revision date, inclusion rule,
and observer.

The complete catalogue also states that its thematic arrangement reflects
modern perceptions and may mislead about genres and relationships, and that
catalogue numbers for unedited compositions can change. An ETCSL number is
therefore a dated provider locator, not a stable ToS Work or artifact identity
and not an ancient classification.

### Nippur catalogue N2

The Penn Museum record identifies object 29-15-155 as a baked-clay Sumerian
school tablet from Nippur, dated 1900-1600 BCE, measuring 6.2 by 4.1 by 2.3 cm,
and describes it as the Old Babylonian Catalogue from Nippur N2. It also lists
`UM 29-15-155 (cast)` as an Other Number:

- <https://collections.penn.museum/collections/object/521562>

The current CDLI record P255993 uses `UM 29-15-155 (cast)` as its Museum Number
while otherwise classifying the record as a clay literary tablet from Nippur:

- <https://cdli.earth/artifacts/255993>
- <https://cdli.earth/artifacts/255993/json>

The current eBL fragment record also describes UM.29-15-155 as a cast. These
routes do not establish whether every current digital representation and
identifier points to the excavated original, a cast, or a provider-level
conflation of both. N2 therefore remains a named coverage observation in this
slice and receives no new ToS physical-artifact identity.

Samuel Noah Kramer's 1942 article is the classical publication control for N2:

- Kramer, `The Oldest Literary Catalogue: A Sumerian List of Literary
  Compositions Compiled about 2000 B.C.`, DOI
  <https://doi.org/10.2307/1355474>
- CDLI publication record <https://cdli.earth/publications/1787048>

The historical title `oldest` and the description `compiled about 2000 B.C.`
remain publication-era claims, not self-updating present authority.

### Louvre catalogue L

- <https://cdli.earth/artifacts/345372>

No exact Louvre collection page for AO 05393 was found in the official

acquisition history, and image-level rights remain unknown.

Henri de Genouillac's 1930 *Textes religieux sumériens du Louvre*, TCL 15, 28,

### Incipits are not stable Work identifiers

N2 lists works by the beginnings of their first lines. Current CDLI annotation
shows that the string `u4 re-a` can be mapped to several different known
compositions, and other entries also remain ambiguous. An incipit occurrence
is therefore a source-visible string at a position on one tablet. Its mapping
to a Work is a versioned editorial claim, not an immutable identifier or an
automatic equivalence relation.

## 2. Established and field-shaping scholarship

Paul Delnero's 2010 article directly re-evaluates the assumption that the
Nippur and Louvre catalogues list the content and sequence of an advanced
scribal curriculum. Its publisher abstract states the bounded conclusion:
they are inventories, not curricular lists.

- Delnero, `Sumerian Literary Catalogues and the Scribal Curriculum`,
  *Zeitschrift für Assyriologie* 100.1 (2010), 32-55,
  <https://doi.org/10.1515/za.2010.003>
- CDLI bibliographic and artifact relation
  <https://cdli.earth/publications/1776907>

This correction prevents four invalid promotions:

1. inventory order to teaching order for the entire list;
2. surviving entries to a complete literary corpus;
3. frequently copied texts to a canon;
4. one Nippur pattern to all Old Babylonian schools.

Eleanor Robson's House F research and Steve Tinney's work on the curricular
setting of Sumerian literature remain essential context controls. The physical
tablet assemblage supports a substantial and locally structured educational
practice, but it does not prove a uniform city-wide or Mesopotamia-wide
curriculum:

- Robson, `The Tablet House: A Scribal School in Old Babylonian Nippur`
  <https://shs.cairn.info/journal-revue-d-assyriologie-2001-1-page-39?lang=en>
- Tinney, `Texts, Tablets, and Teaching`
  <https://www.penn.museum/sites/expedition/texts-tablets-and-teaching/>

Niek Veldhuis's work on elementary education and later curriculum
reconstruction is retained as a control on the distinction between lexical
training, intermediary exercises, and advanced literary study. These phases
cannot be inferred from an isolated catalogue entry alone.

## 3. Fresh current relevance check

### 2023 state-of-field synthesis

The official open volume *Back to School in Babylonia* presents twenty essays
and 126 catalogue objects centered on House F and calls itself a state-of-the-
art synthesis:

- <https://isac.uchicago.edu/research/publications/ISACMP/isacmp1>

Paul Delnero's chapter `The Decad and Scribal Errors` sharpens, rather than
reverses, the 2010 correction:

- N2 and AO 05393 are probably tablet inventories, not curricular lists for
  the whole advanced phase;
- the same ten incipits occur in the same order at the beginning of both lists;
- a double ruling separates the first ten N2 entries from what follows;
- multi-composition tablets and catch-lines independently corroborate the
  ordering of members of the Decad;
- the first-ten grouping can therefore ground a context-bounded Decad sequence,
  while the remaining catalogue order cannot be promoted to the sequence of an
  entire curriculum.

This gives ToS the correct relation model:

`catalogue inventory` + `local position` + `section boundary` + `independent
copying evidence` can support a bounded grouping hypothesis. `Catalogue order`
alone cannot support universal curriculum, complete corpus, canon, or
chronology.

## Reconciliation decisions

1. No ancient Work called `curricular catalogues of Sumerian literature` is
   created. The known catalogues are materially distinct inventories with
   overlapping but non-identical contents.
2. A provisional provider-independent scholarly composite may coordinate Old
   Babylonian Sumerian literary catalogue witnesses without making them copies
   of one original or members of a closed set.
3. Louvre AO 05393 / CDLI P345372 is the one exact physical member admitted in
   the bounded planting. Its provenience and dimensions remain unknown.
4. The physical AO 05393 artifact and the provider-independent catalogue
   composite receive separate target-bounded discovery records derived from
   the same ordered research pass. This prevents one mixed discovery target
   from silently making a physical artifact identical to a scholarly
   composite.
5. Nippur N2 / P255993 remains provider coverage with an explicit
   original-versus-cast identity hold. No physical ToS artifact ID is minted
   until the representation chain is reconciled.
6. ETCSL's 2002 ancient-catalogues page names eleven Old Babylonian entries,
   while its 2006 complete catalogue names thirteen. Both are dated
   project-surface coverage, not a completeness claim or automatically current
   scholarly edition.
7. An incipit occurrence receives tablet-local position and source provenance.
   Any identification with a composition must preserve editor, version,
   ambiguity, and competing candidates.
8. ETCSL numeric and thematic placement remains a mutable modern project
   assertion; it cannot become a stable ancient ID, genre, or relation by
   ingestion.
9. The first ten N2/L entries can support a provisional `Decad grouping and
   sequence` hypothesis only because independent multi-text tablets and
   catch-lines corroborate it. The rest of either catalogue supplies no
   universal curriculum order.
10. No catalogue entry creates a semantic, historical, authorship, influence,
   chronology, philosophy, graph, or canon claim.

## Rights posture

transliterations, composite scores, line art, images, publications, and
derivatives. The Louvre collection page for AO 05393 and an exact image license
were not found. No CDLI or Louvre content-bearing payload is admitted.

ETCSL text, translation, and project pages retain Oxford copyright and are not
copied. The ISAC volume remains a local research copy under its explicit

## Laboratory route prepared, not executed

The planting prepares a later A/B/C comparison:

- A — the exact physical layout of one authorized catalogue witness, preserving
  columns, sequence, ruled boundaries, damage, erasures, and ambiguity;
- B — ETCSL/CDLI provider representations with exact version and response
  provenance;
- C — Delnero 2010 and the 2023 House F synthesis, testing which curriculum and
  grouping claims survive comparison with independent tablet evidence.

Manual review must inspect the actual tablet or authorized facsimile and the
exact reading layer. A matching incipit string, provider composite ID, green
schema, or stable catalogue position cannot verify a sign reading, Work
identity, teaching sequence, or philosophical interpretation.
