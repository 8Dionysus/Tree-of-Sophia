# A01: CDLI P000015 Source Planting Research

Date: 2026-08-12
Authoring posture: model-authored, review-ready, no human judgment claimed
Scope: public-safe metadata planting only; no source content acquired or published

## Result

The A01 atlas backlog now has its first real source planting. The exact object
is the clay tablet cataloged by CDLI as `P000015`, reported under excavation
numbers `W 12256,i + k + l + o` and museum numbers
`VAT 17858 + VAT 17886`.

It enters ToS as a physical artifact witness, not as a Nietzsche-style Work,
Expression, Edition, or Item. The physical tablet, the mutable CDLI catalog
record, its inscription or transliteration, its modern scholarly composite,
its photographs, and its line art remain separate evidence layers.

The planting fulfills one exact A01 source-anchor backlog row. It does not make
accounting philosophy, assign a stable meaning to a sign, accept a text,
create a semantic object, promote a graph relation, or enter canon.

## 1. Official and classical documentation

### Exact current artifact record

The [CDLI P000015 record](https://cdli.earth/P000015) and exact
[JSON API record](https://cdli.earth/artifacts/15/json) agree on the bounded
identity needed here:

- label: `CDLI Lexical 000023, ex. 091 (P000015)`;
- object: lexical clay tablet;
- reported period: Uruk III, approximately 3200-3000 BC;
- excavation site: Uruk (Warka);
- findspot: `Oe XVI,5`;
- excavation ensemble: `W 12256,i + W 12256,k + W 12256,l + W 12256,o`;
- current repository: Vorderasiatisches Museum Berlin;
- inventory: `VAT 17858 + VAT 17886`;
- dimensions: 101 × 99 × 30 mm;
- genre: Lexical; corpus label `Archaic Vessels & Garments (witness)`;
- language: undetermined;
- linked composite layer: `Q000023` / `P471693`.

CDLI's provider code `qpc` is preserved as provider metadata. ToS normalizes
the actual language status to `und`, because the current record itself says
undetermined. Neither the proto-cuneiform context nor a later Sumerian reading
licenses silently naming the artifact's language Sumerian.

The current [CDLI API documentation](https://cdli.earth/docs/api) exposes
machine-readable artifact and inscription routes. The
[metadata field guide](https://cdli.earth/docs/artifact-metadata-fields)
preserves uncertainty and historical changes instead of treating every field
as timeless truth. The record reports metadata revisions in 2001 and 2023 and
transliteration revisions in 2006, 2007, and 2015. Therefore the live catalog
is a dated digital witness, not the artifact itself.

### Classical publication route

The exact record returns to two primary catalog publications:

- Green and Nissen, *Zeichenliste der archaischen Texte aus Uruk*,
  [ATU 2](https://cdli.earth/publications/1638433), 1987, plate 46;
- Englund and Nissen, *Die lexikalischen Listen der archaischen Texte aus
  Uruk*, [ATU 3](https://cdli.earth/publications/1785922), 1993, plate 66,
  `W 12256i+`.

[Archaic Bookkeeping](https://cdli.earth/publications/1657148) remains an
established contextual synthesis for accounting systems. It is not treated as
proof that P000015 is philosophy or that its signs have fixed translations.
No publication plate or book content was acquired.

## 2. Established top scholarship

[Born and Kelley 2021](https://cdli.earth/articles/cdlb/2021-6) is the most
direct methodological guard for sign indexing. Its frequency tables are
snapshots of a changing corpus and its authors deliberately avoid simple
“translations” because signs can be used in multiple ways. For ToS this means
that a sign ID can stabilize reference without stabilizing one meaning.

[Nissen 2024](https://cdli.earth/articles/cdlj/2024-1) documents how the Uruk
excavations and their records developed under uneven historical conditions.
The P000015 findspot is therefore preserved as a reported catalog assertion,
with its literal note, rather than promoted to a self-interpreting
stratigraphic fact.

These two controls establish a durable rule: exact identifiers are the
foundation for return and comparison, not shortcuts from artifact metadata to
historical or semantic certainty.

## 3. Fresh and current relevance check

Kelley, Cartolano, and Ferrara's article
[“Seals and signs”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/seals-and-signs-tracing-the-origins-of-writing-in-ancient-southwest-asia/B3C2D400F3F80A7A0162D9035C9C2804),
online 5 November 2024 and published in the February 2025 issue of
*Antiquity*, argues for specific relationships between pre-literate seal
motifs and proto-cuneiform signs. It also keeps the extent of that relation
open. It is a relevant frontier control, not evidence for the meaning of an
individual sign on P000015.

Unicode proposal
[L2/25-211](https://www.unicode.org/L2/L2025/25211-proto-cuneiform.pdf) is a
current technical route for future sign identifiers and interchange. It was
deferred in this slice: an encoding proposal can help stabilize machine
identity, but encoding identity is not paleographic identity and never
semantic identity.

The freshest operational evidence is CDLI's live 2026 API, field documentation,
terms, and exact record state. “Fresh” here means directly relevant and
currently checked, not merely bearing a 2026 date.

## Rights and publication boundary

The [CDLI Terms of Use](https://cdli.earth/terms-of-use) were checked before
planting:

- bounded catalog facts are retained with source attribution;
- transliterations and translations have a stated academic-reuse route, but
  none was acquired or stored;
- photographs generally belong to holding institutions;
- line art belongs to its named scholarly creators or publication lineage;
- publication or commercial use of images requires prior written permission.

The tracked packet therefore exposes only public-safe metadata. Photograph,
line art, inscription, transliteration, translation, and composite content
are absent. Future site publication is not authorized by this model-authored
assessment.

## Method experiment route

This source opens a useful A/B/C laboratory without pretending all three
conditions have already run.

| Condition | Material | Current state | Expected quality | Cost and speed pressure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | exact live catalog + API metadata | executed | strong identity and provenance; no source text or sign semantics | lowest cost, seconds of machine access, no human work |
| B | exact versioned inscription/transliteration | not run | enables sign occurrence and collation work, still interpretation-limited | moderate acquisition, normalization, and review cost |
| C | artifact photograph, line art, and primary publication plates | not run | strongest visual/paleographic control | highest rights, permission, image, and expert-review cost |

Condition A is not a weak placeholder. It establishes the identities and
separations that B and C must cite. B and C must be judged by direct manual
inspection of their outputs and failure cases, not only by validators.
