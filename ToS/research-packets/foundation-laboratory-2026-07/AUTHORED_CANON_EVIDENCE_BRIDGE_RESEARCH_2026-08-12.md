# Authored canon ↔ evidence-spine bridge research

Research snapshot: 2026-08-12

Status: question-scoped application of the current foundation research; no
canon promotion, German acceptance, translation acceptance, or human review
is created by this packet

## Question

Tree of Sophia already has a living authored `prologue-1` route: twelve
aligned German, Russian, and English segments, Dionysus translation witnesses,
translation tensions, canonical nodes, and 125 route-local relations. The new
source-first foundation separately requires immutable text layers, explicit
segmentation identity, first-class claims, competence-scoped review, and graph
edges that return to evidence.

How can those two histories be joined without either deleting the authored
seed or silently relabelling older authored/review records as modern
philological, translation, semantic, or graph proof?

## 1. Official and classical foundation

This slice applies, rather than duplicates, the official/classical layer
already refreshed on 2026-08-11 in
`SEMANTIC_IDENTITY_ANNOTATION_RESEARCH_2026-08-11.md` and
`SOURCE_TEXT_UNIT_SEGMENTATION_RESEARCH_2026-08-11.md`:

- W3C Web Annotation keeps source state and selector separate from annotation
  identity. A legacy `[n]` label can therefore remain an authored locator while
  a new exact text-unit ID and selector provide source return.
- TEI P5 stand-off analysis permits multiple incompatible or overlapping
  structures over one immutable representation. The printed TEI paragraphs
  and the authored twelve-segment reading do not need to be forced into one
  segmentation.
- PROV and CIDOC CRM/CRMinf distinguish a source, an assignment or
  interpretation act, its evidence, and later adoption. A dated review note
  does not become a modern machine-readable human attestation merely because
  it is in `ToS/review-ledger/`.
- SKOS and OntoLex keep labels, lexical entities, signs, and concepts apart.
  Existing names such as `return-as-action` remain authored handles; they are
  not automatically `sign_id`, `lexeme_id`, or accepted semantic claims.

## 2. Established work

The established layer from the same research packets supplies the migration
law:

- Sanderson, Ciccarese, and Van de Sompel's Open Annotation design supports a
  portable stand-off bridge rather than editing source bytes or embedding new
  authority in an old node.
- Groth, Gibson, and Velterop's nanopublication separation supports keeping
  assertion, provenance, and publication context distinct.
- Kuhn and Dumontier's trusty-URI work supports digest-bound immutable route
  snapshots, while not making a digest evidence that an interpretation is
  true.
- Artstein and Poesio's annotation-agreement distinctions, applied through
  `HUMAN_ASSURANCE_RESEARCH.md`, prohibit inferring a real-human or
  inter-reviewer assurance level from an untyped historical review note.

## 3. Fresh relevant evidence

The 2025–2026 layer was refreshed one day before this application and is still
the current owner research at execution time. It shows why this bridge must be
non-promoting:

- Tsaneva et al. (2025) support selective human verification instead of
  treating standalone model or graph validation as sufficient.
- Hagen (2025) and Zhao, Siro, and Hollink (2026) preserve the competence gap
  for historical-language semantics and figurative use.
- Kasner et al., Kulmizev et al., and Kim et al. (2026) do not justify
  substituting model ensembles or synthetic annotators for the absent human
  evidence.
- transparent 2026 co-occurrence work supports retaining exact recurrence and
  context as evidence, not promoting them to a stable sign.

No newer source is needed to answer the bounded reconciliation question. The
freshness dependency is explicit: a future change to the semantic or
source-unit owner law must re-open the research rather than relying on this
dated application note.

## 4. Exact repository evidence

The exact DTA Part-I TEI contains twelve `<p>` elements in
`Za-I-Vorrede-1`. A direct, no-network comparison against the canonical
authored source node found:

- twelve German authored segments;
- one unique ordered match for every segment;
- complete normalized coverage of the section;
- a deliberate authored boundary between segments 5 and 6 inside the fifth
  TEI paragraph;
- a later TEI paragraph boundary inside authored segment 6;
- only declared presentation differences: line whitespace, DTA's `U+00AC`
  discretionary line-break marker, the donor's visible hyphen-at-line-break,
  and Markdown emphasis markers.

This proves a deterministic representation crosswalk. It does **not** prove
German correctness, translation fidelity, sign identity, interpretive truth,
or the adequacy of the old review process.

## 5. Contract consequence

The bridge must therefore:

1. extract the complete exact DTA section to an immutable private raw layer;
2. create a separate private editorial-normalized comparison layer with an
   explicit, digest-bound operation receipt;
3. retain two reciprocal segmentations over that frozen layer:
   source-attested TEI paragraphs and imported authored-route segments;
4. bind the source node, donor, canonical nodes, relation pack, and relevant
   review notes by exact digest;
5. record all 125 old relations as authored canon whose modern claim/evidence
   closure is still zero;
6. leave every older node and edge in place while prohibiting automatic bulk
   migration or graph admission;
7. create no human task merely to make a validator green.

The result is a bridge and debt inventory, not a demotion of lived authorship
and not a promotion into the new semantic contract.

## Stop line

The exact twelve-segment crosswalk may support later question-triggered claim
or translation packets. It cannot itself accept the German reading, the
Dionysus translations, any sign, concept, relation, interpretation, graph
edge, canon revision, public payload, or server transfer.
