## Exact backlog pressure

This packet addresses line 26 of the A02 branch-local source backlog:
`Claus Wilcke, Laws of Lipit-Ishtar`, described there as an anchor for the
early legal line and its structure.

The label is bibliographically compressed and its source type
`монография/издание` is not exact. It points most literally to one short
modern Work, but the edition-level contribution expected by the backlog is
supplied by another:

1. Claus Wilcke, `Laws of Lipit-Ishtar`, an entry in *The Oxford
   Encyclopedia of the Bible and Law* (Oxford University Press, 2015);
2. Claus Wilcke, `Gesetze in sumerischer Sprache`, a chapter in *Studies in
   Sumerian Language and Literature: Festschrift Joachim Krecher*, Babel und
   Bibel 8, whose Lipit-Ištar section is the direct critical control.

These are two modern Works with different purposes. The encyclopedia entry is
a concise reference treatment. The German chapter presents and discusses
Sumerian law materials, including the Lipit-Ištar reconstruction. Neither is
the ancient Laws of Lipit-Ištar Work, one physical tablet, the current
scholarly composite, an exact Edition or Item, or timeless ground truth. One
backlog line therefore receives two plantings rather than one invented hybrid.

The ancient foundation already exists and is reused, not recreated:

## 2. Established and field-shaping scholarship

Wilcke's chapter is itself the established critical anchor being planted. Its
CDLI relation covers both the Laws of Ur-Namma and the Laws of Lipit-Ištar,
while the exact Lipit-Ištar portion is 573-602. That editorial coordinate
belongs to Wilcke's modern Work; it does not become an intrinsic address of
the ancient Work or of every physical witness.

Wilcke's own `Corrigenda zu Claus Wilcke, "Gesetze in sumerischer Sprache"`,
NABU 2015/1 no. 6, pages 6-7, records concrete corrections after publication:

- <https://sepoa.fr/wp/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/NABU-_2015-1_DEF-.pdf>

The existence of authorial corrigenda is direct evidence that even the
critical anchor must remain versioned and correction-aware. The freely
viewable NABU PDF is used only as web evidence in this packet; no copy is
admitted to ToS and no blanket redistribution conclusion is inferred.

Martha T. Roth's *Law Collections from Mesopotamia and Asia Minor* remains the
standard cross-corpus English translation and reference control. It has its
own separate A02 source planting at line 24 and is not silently absorbed into
Wilcke. Raymond Westbrook and Pamela Barmash remain controls for the boundary
between law collection, royal inscription, scribal tradition, and modern
legislation theory. None of them turns one scholarly reconstruction into the
ancient source.
