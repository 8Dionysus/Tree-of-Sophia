# A02 Old Babylonian Gilgamesh fragments: source-planting research

Date of evidence cut: 2026-08-22

Status: source-identity and rights research completed for one metadata-only
planting. No tablet image, transliteration, reconstruction, translation, scan,
or scholarly publication body is admitted by this packet.

## Exact backlog pressure

This packet addresses line 17 of the A02 branch-local source backlog:
`Old Babylonian Gilgamesh fragments`, described there as a primary epic corpus
whose existence is highly reliable but whose completeness is only moderately
reliable. The source need is not satisfied by calling one modern translation
or one museum tablet *the* Old Babylonian epic.

The bounded result separates:

- a provider-independent Work identity for the Old Babylonian Gilgamesh
  tradition;
- the physical tablet Penn CBS 07771 / CDLI P262784;
- a current scholarly synoptic composite spanning eBL's Old Babylonian units;
- the mutable provider records, manuscript lists, transliterations,
  translations, photographs, and critical editions;
- every later semantic, historical, and philosophical claim.

## 1. Official and classical documentation

### Exact physical witnesses

The official Penn Museum record identifies CBS 7771 as a clay Akkadian tablet,
approximately 18.5 by 16.5 by 2.4 cm, dated about 1650 BCE, purchased in 1917,
and described as a nearly complete three-column second tablet of the Gilgamesh
Epic. Penn reports `Warka (uncertain)` as provenience and also preserves a CBS
register note naming Sippar:

- <https://www.penn.museum/collections/object/245958>

The current CDLI record identifies the same physical object as P262784 / CBS
07771, Old Babylonian, Akkadian, clay, and held by the Penn Museum, but reports
Nippur as provenience:

- <https://cdli.earth/artifacts/262784>
- <https://cdli.earth/artifacts/262784/json>

The current eBL manuscript and fragment routes identify the object as
CBS.7771 and bind it to Old Babylonian unit II:

- <https://www.ebl.lmu.de/api/texts/L/1/4/chapters/Old%20Babylonian/II/manuscripts>
- <https://www.ebl.lmu.de/api/fragments/CBS.7771>

These provider routes do not reconcile their locality statements. The physical
route therefore uses `uncertain/`; Warka, Sippar, Nippur, and the broader eBL
label Babylonia remain source-attributed observations rather than one promoted
findspot.

Yale's official Babylonian Collection highlights YBC 2178 as another Old
Babylonian Gilgamesh tablet, concerning preparation for the Cedar Forest and
Huwawa. It is retained as an independent control, not merged with CBS 7771:

- <https://babylonian-collection.yale.edu/highlights>
- <https://cdli.earth/artifacts/273176/history/artifact>

### Classical publication controls

Stephen Langdon's 1917 *The Epic of Gilgamish* is the initial publication
route for the Penn tablet and remains available through ETANA:

- <https://etana.org/sites/default/files/coretexts/14909.pdf>

Morris Jastrow Jr. and Albert T. Clay's 1920 *An Old Babylonian Version of the
Gilgamesh Epic* is the classical Pennsylvania/Yale comparison. Project
Gutenberg identifies its US digital edition as public domain and last updated
it on 2024-10-28:

- <https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/11000>

These works are historical controls. Their editorial joins, readings,
translations, and conclusions do not become current text authority merely
because their publication bodies are openly available.

## 3. Fresh current relevance check

### Live eBL representation

The official LMU project page states that eBL's corpus editions are
continuously updated as research changes and that AI assists fragment
identification. The corpus and Fragmentarium were publicly released in
February 2023:

- <https://www.assyriologie.uni-muenchen.de/forschung/ebl/index.html>

The live eBL metadata API was checked on 2026-08-22. It currently calls the
cross-period Work `Poem of Gilgameš`, reports approximately 3,150 verses for
the overall corpus, and exposes fourteen Old Babylonian editorial units:

`II`, `III`, `UM`, `Schøyen₁`, `Schøyen₂`, `Schøyen₃`, `Nippur`, `Harmal₁`,
`Harmal₂`, `Ishchali`, `IM`, `VA+BM`, `CUNES`, and `SM`.

All fourteen manuscript endpoints were queried. Together they returned 21
current manuscript records: one each for thirteen units and eight records in
`Schøyen₃`. This is a dated coverage observation, not a completeness claim.
The current API itself characterizes the Old Babylonian evidence as
miscellaneous and warns against treating it as one fixed recension:

- <https://www.ebl.lmu.de/api/texts/L/1/4>

The exact 2026-08-22 response fingerprints used for reproducibility are stored
in the physical and composite witness records. No API body is retained in the
repository.

The eBL API and frontend software repositories use the MIT license. That
software license does not authorize the corpus editions, translations,
transliterations, photographs, or contributed scholarly content. No such
content is admitted here.

### Current Penn rights and dataset state

Penn's current data page says that downloadable object metadata datasets are
CC BY 4.0, exclude images, were last updated 2026-08-12, and total 144.20 MB:

- <https://collections.penn.museum/collections/objects/data.php>

Penn separately requires contact for high-resolution or publication-quality
object images and for publication permission. The current planting therefore
uses attributed object metadata only and neither downloads nor publishes an
image:

- <https://www.penn.museum/about-collections/rights-and-permissions>

### Recency rejection

The 2025 KASKAL article `More Gilgameš Fragments` is newer by calendar date,
but the directly described fragments belong to the Standard Babylonian
sequence. It is not used to inflate the Old Babylonian witness count. Recent
cuneiform OCR and sign-detection work is relevant to a future laboratory, not
to the identity of this source route.

## Reconciliation decisions

1. `Old Babylonian Gilgamesh tradition` is a provisional Work identity. It is
   neither the five Sumerian Gilgamesh poems nor the later Standard Babylonian
   poem, and it is not asserted to be one author-final text.
2. CBS 7771 receives a provider-independent physical identity under an
   uncertain-site path. Provider disagreement is preserved, not averaged.
3. The eBL Old Babylonian stage becomes a provisional synoptic scholarly
   composite. Its fourteen units and 21 manuscript records are dated mutable
   coverage observations.
4. CBS 7771 is the one exact physical member admitted in this slice. Other
   eBL records remain named coverage, not newly minted ToS artifact identities.
5. No authorship claim is created. Ancient transmission, copyist activity,
   editorial reconstruction, and modern scholarly responsibility are separate.
6. No source text, edition, Expression, Item, translation, semantic claim,
   graph fact, or canon object is created.

## Rights posture

The tracked layer may publish the bounded attributed metadata records. Penn's
CC BY 4.0 dataset statement is limited to object metadata and explicitly
excludes images. CDLI and eBL source-bearing content, modern transliterations,
critical reconstruction, translations, photographs, line art, and publication
bodies retain separate rights and provenance requirements.

The ancient age of the tablets establishes no blanket right over modern
editorial or visual layers. Local research access never silently becomes
future-site publication authority.

## Laboratory route prepared, not executed

The planting prepares a later A/B/C comparison:

- A — one exact physical witness and its source-visible layout;
- B — the current eBL Old Babylonian synoptic units with version provenance;
- C — George 2003 plus later exact fragments and competing editorial readings.

Manual checks must inspect the tablet image or authorized facsimile, the exact
transliteration version, and disagreements among witnesses. A matching ID,
green schema, or stable eBL coordinate cannot verify an Akkadian reading or
semantic interpretation.

## Manual audit limits

This model-made packet manually reconciles metadata, provider scope, current
counts, and rights statements. It performs no Akkadian-language review, no
cuneiform sign inspection, no collation, no translation review, and no legal
review. Those absent competencies remain explicit rather than being replaced
with a validator result.
