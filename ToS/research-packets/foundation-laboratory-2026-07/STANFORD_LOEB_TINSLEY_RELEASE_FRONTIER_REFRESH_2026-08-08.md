## Research order

### I. Official publisher documentation

The current [Stanford University Press title page](https://www.sup.org/books/theory-and-philosophy/thus-spoke-zarathustra)
identifies Complete Works volume 7, Friedrich Nietzsche, translators Paul S.
Loeb and David F. Tinsley, August 2026, 424 pages, cloth ISBN
`9780804728799`, and paper ISBN `9781503647282`. It announces editorial
cross-references to notebook drafts and variants, a translators' afterword on
translation problems, and a key-term glossary. Those are declared contents,
not inspected contents or passage-level quality evidence.

The official [Spring/Summer 2026 catalog](https://supress.sites-pro.stanford.edu/sites/supress/files/media/file/spring26_catalog.pdf)
repeats August 2026 and 424 pages and adds eBook ISBN `9781503647299`.
Because the publisher does not give an exact day on either surface, ToS does
not promote a retailer-supplied day into publisher-confirmed publication.

The publisher's [copy-request route](https://www.sup.org/books/theory-and-philosophy/thus-spoke-zarathustra/desk-examination-copy-requests)
is purpose-bound: review copies are for media planning a review, while desk
and examination copies are instructor routes. ToS is a solo+AI research
project, not a publication or adopted course. No request was sent and this
page is not treated as general permission to acquire or reuse the work.

### II. Established scholarly lineage

The same translators previously produced Stanford Complete Works volumes
[14](https://www.sup.org/books/theory-and-philosophy/unpublished-fragments-period-thus-spoke-zarathustra-summer-1882-winter)
and [15](https://www.sup.org/books/theory-and-philosophy/unpublished-fragments-period-thus-spoke-zarathustra-spring-1884-winter),
covering notebook material from the period of *Zarathustra*. Stanford's
edition note says that the series follows the Colli–Montinari source route,
incorporates corrections, seeks consistent terminology, and adds annotation
and afterwords.

Robin Small's 2020 [review of volume 14 in the *Journal of Nietzsche
Studies*](https://doi.org/10.5325/jnietstud.51.1.0133) establishes that the
earlier Loeb–Tinsley work received specialist review and is directly relevant
to Nietzsche scholarship. This makes volume 7 a high-value candidate. It does
not transfer a quality verdict from a different volume, prove the new
translation's accuracy, or make either English expression ground truth.
