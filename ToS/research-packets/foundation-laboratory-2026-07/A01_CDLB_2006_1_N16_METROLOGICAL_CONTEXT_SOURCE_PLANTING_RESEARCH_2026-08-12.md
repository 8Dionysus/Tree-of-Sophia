### Classical numerical substrate

Monaco explicitly depends on the numerical systems reconstructed by Peter
Damerow and Robert K. Englund in *Die Zahlzeichensysteme der Archaischen Texte
aus Uruk*, published inside ATU 2 in 1987. Footnote 11 uses their approximate
`N1 = barig = 24 litres` relation. Under that convention, the article's
`1/100 barig` result is approximately one quarter litre.

This dependency must remain dimensional and contextual:

- `N1`, `N39`, `N24`, `N29`, and `N16` are sign identities or scholarly
  notations, not bare scalar numbers;
- the numerical relation is evaluated inside a capacity system and an
  accounting case;
- the approximate litre conversion is a modern model, not content written on
  the tablet;
- a sign's place in one metrological system is not automatically its value in
  every context.

ATU 2 is therefore the classical metrological control, not a substitute for
the tablet editions on which Monaco's deductions operate.

### The exact source argument

The three-page article begins from a reported total of three known N16
attestations and adds better-preserved, though partly unprovenanced, tablets.
Its reasoning proceeds through several distinct evidential moves:

1. In MS 4499 / CDLI `P006303`, a barley account with `TARa` is interpreted
   through a ten-percent tribute relation. An initial equation would make
   N16 equal `0.015 barig`, after supplying a missing `3N39a`.
2. In CUNES 50-06-203 / `P283918`, a monthly beer-ration calculation yields
   `0.15 barig = 1N24 + 1N29a + 1N16`, from which Monaco derives
   `1N16 = 0.01 barig = 1/100 barig`.
3. The article then rejects `0.015` as the preferred result and treats the
   MS 4499 tribute as rounded to `3.61 barig`.
4. CUNES 50-06-217 / `P283919` supplies a second contextual check through a
   proposed relation between 240 small cereal products and `2.4 barig` of
   grain groats.
5. CUNES 51-10-007 / `P283917` contains a possible N16 occurrence whose value
   the article says cannot be determined from that context.
6. W 21208,8+ / `P000524` places N16 and N17 in sequence in a lexical list.
   Monaco proposes `1/200 barig` for N17 only as a hypothesis awaiting future
   textual evidence.

The initial `0.015` is therefore not the article's conclusion. The final
`1/100 barig` is not obtained from the sign shape alone; it depends on
case-level arithmetic, readings, restorations, the ten-percent interpretation
of `TARa`, a metrological conversion, and comparison across tablets.

## 2. Established scholarship and methodological controls

### Contextual reconstruction is a claim chain

The article is useful to ToS because it exposes a reconstructive chain rather
than merely assigning a gloss. A future claim packet for this kind of result
must be able to separate at least:

- the physical tablet and current catalog identity;
- the exact transcription version and case address;
- observed, damaged, restored, corrected, and editorially supplied signs;
- the source-reported commodity and administrative qualification;
- the numerical system and unit-conversion model;
- each equation, rounding rule, and assumed ratio;
- corroborating, contradictory, and indeterminate cases;
- the proposed sign value, its scope, and its review history.

A compact `N16 -> 1/100 barig` edge would erase the method that makes the
proposal inspectable. A generic `N16 means 1/100` edge would be worse: it
would discard the capacity unit, period, corpus, and accounting context.

### CUSAS 1 and current Cornell records

Monaco's 2007 *The Cornell University Archaic Tablets* (CUSAS 1) later
published the Cornell witnesses that the 2005 PDF described as forthcoming.
Current CDLI artifact pages preserve the relevant coordinates:

- `P283918`, CUSAS 1 no. 143 / CUNES 50-06-203, retains two N16 occurrences
  and a catalog calculation `N16 = 1/20 N39`;
- `P283919`, CUSAS 1 no. 112 / CUNES 50-06-217, retains N16 in the paired
  product/grain-groats cases and the same `1/20 N39` calculation;
- `P283917`, CUSAS 1 no. 172 / CUNES 51-10-007, retains N16 without making
  that one case decisive;
- `P000524`, ATU 3 pl. 75 / W 21208,8+, retains the lexical N16/N17 sequence.

These records strengthen source return, but their modern catalog notes do not
turn one interpretation into immutable tablet content. The artifact, image,
transcription, calculation note, article argument, and later sign-list entry
remain different layers.

### Journal scope is a built-in limitation

CDLI describes the Bulletin as a refereed venue for short, specific notes of
at most five pages rather than synthetic treatments of complex subjects. That
matches the A01 control label: this is a specialized case study, not a full
theory of proto-cuneiform semantics, metrology, or sign identity. Peer review
raises its evidential weight but does not remove the need for source-visible
adjudication.

## 3. Fresh current relevance check

### A decisive current-edition drift in P006303

The live `P006303` transcription no longer contains N16 in the tribute case.
Its approved CDLI history records that on 12 February 2018 Robert K. Englund
replaced:

```text
3(N01)# 3(N39)#? 1(N16) , TAR~a
```

with:

```text
3(N01)# 3(N39~a)#? 1(N24) , TAR~a
```

The live page now displays N24. Consequently:

- Monaco's first reconstruction must be reproduced against the dated
  transcription available to the article, not silently against the current
  edition;
- a current rerun must treat the P006303 N16 premise as superseded by the live
  CDLI reading unless a new source-visible review disputes that revision;
- the remaining Cornell and lexical attestations must be evaluated
  independently rather than borrowing support from P006303;
- ToS must retain edition history as evidence, because a stable artifact ID
  does not imply a stable transcription or sign occurrence.

This is exactly why sign value, occurrence, transcription, and artifact
identity cannot be collapsed.

### Current PCSL identity and frequency surface

The current Proto-Cuneiform Sign List exposes:

- current sign ID `o0981940` for `1(N16)`;
- a mutable frequency-page count of `9` when checked on 12 August 2026;
- LLATU concordance entry `324_06` and an older list coordinate
  `o0902960`;
- an internal/private-use coordinate `U+F00FB` on the sign page.

The count is a dated PCSL surface observation, not a ToS occurrence count and
not proof that nine independent physical tablets carry an accepted N16
reading. PCSL's frequency page does not expose in-place the complete corpus,
version, normalization, damaged-sign, and counting policy required for direct
comparison with Monaco's reported attestations. The old and current OIDs are
also list coordinates, not semantic equivalence by themselves.

### Unicode status on 12 August 2026

Unicode 17.0, released in September 2025, is the current final standard and
does not encode proto-cuneiform numerals. Unicode 18.0 is still a beta/draft
with formal release planned for 16 September 2026. Its archaic-cuneiform
repertoire and character names are stable at the beta stage, but the standard
itself is not final.

An earlier 2023 proposal, L2/23-190, included a proposed N16 character. The
official Unicode 18.0 beta `UnicodeData.txt` checked on 12 August 2026 contains
no N16 entry, even though it includes the new Archaic Cuneiform Numerals block
`U+12550..U+1268F`. Therefore:

- N16 has no final Unicode character in Unicode 17.0;
- it is also absent from the stable Unicode 18.0 beta repertoire checked here;
- PCSL `U+F00FB` is a private-use implementation coordinate, not a portable
  Unicode identity;
- older proposal code points and names must not be planted as current facts.

This exclusion is not evidence against Monaco's metrological argument.
Encoding eligibility, graphemic identity, transcription practice, contextual
value, and semantics answer different questions.

### Fresh literature boundary

A targeted 2024–2026 search found no later peer-reviewed paper specifically
re-adjudicating N16's metrological value. The most relevant fresh controls are
therefore the current CDLI editions and their revision history, current PCSL
identity/frequency surfaces, and the 2026 Unicode pipeline. Generic cuneiform
OCR, sign-image classification, and broad undeciphered-script papers were
excluded because they do not test the article's exact tablets, equations, and
edition history.

Absence of a located later paper is a search result, not a completeness claim.

## Laboratory consequences

The smallest honest A/B/C design is:

- **A — historical reconstruction:** freeze Monaco's PDF version, its cited
  transcription states, equations, restorations, and unit assumptions; verify
  that the reported intermediate and final calculations can be reproduced.
- **B — current-edition reconstruction:** rerun the same reasoning from dated
  current CDLI transcriptions, explicitly applying the 2018 P006303 N24
  revision and recording which conclusions survive.
- **C — adversarial sensitivity:** vary one premise at a time: remove each
  tablet, retain or reject uncertain occurrences, change the TARa ten-percent
  assumption, rounding policy, N39/N1 relation, reading/restoration, and PCSL
  corpus membership.

Every arm must record source version, exact case addresses, arithmetic,
runtime, elapsed time, cost, and disagreements. Manual checking must inspect
the visible tablet evidence where rights and access permit, the historical
and current transcription diff, and the actual calculation chain. A green
schema, matching scalar result, LLM consensus, or current PCSL count does not
establish the intended reading or semantics.

Useful evaluation dimensions are:

- source-return precision and ability to expose superseded readings;
- arithmetic correctness and dimensional consistency;
- sensitivity to each uncertain premise;
- distinction among observation, restoration, hypothesis, and conclusion;
- time, compute, and human attention required per adjudicated case.

Runtime and local-model experiments belong in the `abyss-stack` laboratory;
ToS retains the source identity, research design, evidence route, and eventual
review outcomes.
