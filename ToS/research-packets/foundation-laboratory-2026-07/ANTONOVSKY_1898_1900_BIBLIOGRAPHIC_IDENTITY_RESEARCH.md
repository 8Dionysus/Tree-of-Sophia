# Antonovsky 1898–1900 Bibliographic-Identity Research

Date of evidence refresh: 2026-08-01
Scope: the first Russian publication of *Also sprach Zarathustra* associated
with Antonovsky, the separately issued 1898 state, the uncredited 1899
*Reader* edition, and the first 1900 edition counted by Antonovsky himself
Authority posture: model-made bibliographic research and direct source-image
inspection; no human bibliography, collation, translation-quality, semantic,
or rights review

## Result first

The statements “Antonovsky's translation first appeared in 1898” and “the
first of Antonovsky's four editions was published in 1900” are compatible.
They refer to different bibliographic boundaries:

1. In 1898 the translation appeared through F. I. Bulgakov's *New Journal of
   Foreign Literature, Art and Science*. Antonovsky's 1911 preface calls the
   1898 appearances excerpts. Surviving journal contents and a source-visible
   separately paginated title page establish a journal/supplement publication
   state; the inspected title page dates it 1898 and names the journal's
   editorial office as publisher.
2. In 1899 the Moscow journal *Reader* issued a separate, 236-page book without
   naming Antonovsky. The current RNL/RuNEB record identifies the exact
   edition. A contemporary 1901 source says the translation had been reprinted
   from the *New Journal* and issued as a separate book without Antonovsky's
   permission or name.
3. In his 1911 preface Antonovsky counts his four editions as 1900, 1903, 1907,
   and 1911. The 1900 state therefore remains the first edition in his own
   edition sequence, not the first appearance of any part of his translation.

This identity pass itself created no Expression, Edition, Item,
responsibility claim, or derivation edge for 1898 or 1899. A later exact
source-witness pass, recorded in
`READER_1899_FRAGMENT_SOURCE_WITNESS_RESEARCH.md`, lawfully acquired the
official RuNEB return and therefore now materializes a separate provisional
uncredited Russian Expression, a verified 1899 Edition, and a verified but
fragmentary three-part PDF Item. It still creates no translator claim,
1898/1900 derivation, textual equivalence, accepted text, or completeness
claim. The 1898 title-page evidence remains exposed by a commercial holding
rather than an institutionally controlled complete object, the inspected
journal bindings omit the actual *Zarathustra* supplement leaves, and no exact
1900 Item has been acquired. Preserving these limits is more valuable than
prematurely completing the historical chain.

## 1. Classical and official documentation

### 1.1 The exact journal identity

The [Russian National Library / National Electronic Library parent
record](https://rusneb.ru/catalog/000200_000018_RU_NLR_INFOCOMM7_1000052153/)
identifies the periodical as *Новый журнал иностранной литературы, искусства и
науки*, Saint Petersburg, F. I. Bulgakov, 1897–1909. It separately enumerates
the 1898 issues, including volume 4(6), numbers 10, 11, and 12. This corrects a
tempting but false shortcut to the differently titled *Вестник иностранной
литературы*.

The current [cross-library periodical
index](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Novyj_%C5%BEurnal_inostrannoj_literatury%2C_iskusstva_i_nauki)
routes the 1898 volumes to Penn State, Google Books, and Internet Archive. Four
Internet Archive OCR volumes were inspected as discovery evidence. Their
contents pages establish the serial route:

- June 1898, number 6: *Такъ говорилъ Заратустра. Мистическая поэма Фридриха
  Ницше. Съ нѣмецкаго переводъ Ю. М. Антоновскаго*, pages 17–32;
- July 1898, number 7: the same title and responsibility, but the OCR damages
  the first digit of the displayed page range;
- August 1898, number 8: the same title and responsibility, with another
  OCR-damaged rendering of the range;
- October 1898, number 10: a special supplement entry for *Такъ говорилъ
  Заратустра*, pages 49–64.

The inspected Internet Archive bindings expose contents metadata but omit the
actual *Zarathustra* supplement leaves. The first 1–16 range and the exact
November/December continuation have not been directly observed in those
files. No start month, completion month, or complete serial extent is therefore
accepted from sequence alone.

### 1.2 The source-visible 1898 separate state

A June 2025 auction record exposes one high-resolution photograph containing
the physical binding, title page, and opening page. The title page visibly
states:

- *Такъ говорилъ Заратустра*;
- *Книга для всѣхъ и ни для кого*;
- *Символическая поэма Фридриха Ницше*;
- translation from the tenth German edition;
- the displayed responsibility `М. Ю. Антоновскаго`;
- edition of the editorial office of the *New Journal of Foreign Literature*;
- Saint Petersburg, 1898.

The [auction description](https://www.auction-imperia.ru/book/120_5.htm)
reports 90 pages, illustrations, and a 24.2 × 17.2 cm format. The direct image
is stronger than the cataloguer's normalized prose for what is printed on the
page. In particular, the source really does display reversed initials `М. Ю.`;
they must not be silently rewritten as though the title page displayed
`Ю. М.`. Antonovsky's own later testimony and the already reconciled authority
chain support the person identity, while the historical literal remains a
separate observation.

This photograph proves that a separately paginated 1898 journal-office state
existed. It does not supply a stable institutional Item, complete page-level
return, file fixity for the whole book, or redistribution permission. The
current ToS result is therefore a discovery lead and research finding, not a
new corpus object.

### 1.3 Antonovsky's own edition history

Direct visual inspection of the fixity-bound 1911 RSL/RuNEB Item, PDF page 4,
shows Antonovsky's preface. He says excerpts of his translation first appeared
in Bulgakov's journal in 1898. He then names four editions of his translation:
1900, 1903, 1907, and the present 1911. His statement that each of the three
previous editions was a new reworking rather than a simple reprint already
supports the admitted 1903→1900 and 1907→1903 claims. It does not say that the
1900 Expression is a direct revision of one exact 1898 Expression.

The exact 2007 *Cultural Revolution* Edition supplies a different but
compatible statement: the Antonovsky translation, first published in 1898,
was taken as the basis, carefully checked, and newly edited. “First published
in 1898” identifies historical origin; it does not turn the 1898 publication
state into an exact source endpoint for the 2007 Expression.

### 1.4 The official 1899 *Reader* Edition

The current [RNL/RuNEB
record](https://rusneb.ru/catalog/000200_000018_v19_rc_1709542/) identifies:

- *Так говорил Заратустра: символическая поэма, перевод с немецкого*;
- Moscow, edition of the editorial office of the journal *Reader*, 1899;
- 236 pages, 18 cm;
- offprint from *Reader* 1899, books 16, 18–20, 26–28, and 30–35;
- no translator responsibility in the title statement.

The record exposes a fragmentary digital copy. A later exact-object pass used
the official unauthenticated ZIP route once and received three fixity-bound
PDF members totaling 22 photographed surfaces, not the complete cataloged 236
pages. The current RNL shelfmark remains unresolved. Open viewing and download
availability are access evidence, not a reusable-content license for every
digital layer.

### 1.5 Contemporary 1901 reception as lineage evidence

The official KazNEB copy of Henri Lichtenberger's *Философия Ницше*, Saint
Petersburg, O. N. Popova, 1901, includes a Russian introductory essay by M.
Nevedomsky. Direct source-image inspection shows two relevant statements:

- the page-IV footnote says excerpts from *Zarathustra* in Antonovsky's
  translation were printed in the *New Journal* in 1898;
- page V says the separately issued book had been reprinted from the *New
  Journal* by a “commercial” publishing method, without Antonovsky's
  permission and without mention of his name.

The essay later cites page 89 of the edition of the journal *Reader*, joining
that criticism to the official 1899 Edition identity. The source is
contemporary and specific, but it is a critical report rather than collation.
It establishes reported historical dependence and the uncredited publication
boundary; it does not prove line-for-line identity.

## 2. Established scholarship

The 1911 *New Encyclopedic Dictionary* [entry for
Antonovsky](https://ru.wikisource.org/wiki/%D0%9D%D0%AD%D0%A1/%D0%90%D0%BD%D1%82%D0%BE%D0%BD%D0%BE%D0%B2%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B9,_%D0%AE%D0%BB%D0%B8%D0%B9_%D0%9C%D0%B8%D1%85%D0%B0%D0%B9%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%87)
identifies Antonovsky as a Nietzsche translator and records the fourth Saint
Petersburg edition of 1911. A peer-reviewed 2020 Institute of Philosophy RAS
[study](https://pj.iphras.ru/issue/download/307/144) independently places his
Nietzsche translations in the early Russian reception history. Pertsev's 2015
[study of Russian *Zarathustra*
translations](https://doi.org/10.17072/2078-7898/2015-3-24-31) demonstrates why
meaning, rhythm, style, and translation strategy must be compared at the
witness level. Boiko's 2007 [account of the Cultural Revolution
edition](https://www.ng.ru/koncep/2007-09-13/10_zaratustra.html) reports the
1898 origin and later editorial intervention.

These established sources corroborate the broad history but do not replace
the exact 1898, 1899, or 1900 witnesses. Vocabulary also varies between
“excerpts,” “supplement,” and “separate edition.” That conflict is retained
rather than normalized into one unsupported object type.

The established version-history principle remains decisive: publication
history is evidence of relation pressure, not proof that two textual states
are interchangeable. The 90-page 1898 large-format state, the 236-page 1899
small-format state, and the currently uninspected 1900 Edition cannot be
collapsed from pagination, title, or translator attribution.

## 3. Fresh and current evidence

L. L. Ermakova's 2025 article on Vyacheslav Ivanov's draft
[translation](https://publications.hse.ru/en/articles/1061726305) compares
Russian *Zarathustra* translations, including Antonovsky, through variants,
rhetorical form, rhythm, archaism, and translation strategy. It does not settle
the 1898–1900 bibliography, but it is current confirmation that exact witness
identity must precede meaningful translation comparison.

The current official HSE metadata assigns that named article pages 170–177
and DOI `10.17588/2076-9210.2025.2.170-177`. Pages 161–169 and the DOI ending
`161-169` belong to the related article *Неизвестный перевод Вяч.
Иванова из Ницше*, not to the article named above. This pass records the
correction rather than inheriting the adjacent identifier.

Current RNL/RuNEB pages were rechecked on 2026-08-01. Their live records still
identify the Bulgakov periodical and the 1899 *Reader* Edition. Currentness
supports route availability only; it does not strengthen historical claims
beyond the records' actual fields.

## 4. General web last

General web search was used only after the official, primary, and established
routes. Marketplace descriptions independently report a 90-page 1898
separate state, and one surviving auction photograph exposes the exact title
page. Those results are valuable discovery evidence because the physical page
is visible. They remain weaker than an originating institutional catalog plus
a stable digital object for identity, access, and rights.

Marketplace prose that calls the object the complete “first Russian
translation” is not accepted. Contemporary testimony calls the 1898
appearances excerpts, and later books add unresolved textual-state questions.
The source image is retained as evidence of the displayed title page, not as
authority for completeness, quality, or a canonical first text.

## Rights and local/public boundary

The underlying Nietzsche Work and Antonovsky's historical translation age do
not automatically settle rights in a particular scan, photograph, editorial
state, repository object, or metadata export. For every acquired object ToS
must continue to separate:

- underlying Work rights;
- translation rights;
- Edition/editorial rights;
- physical-object and reproduction rights;
- repository access terms;
- local computational use;
- public redistribution and future-site publication.

Local-only research use may proceed on lawfully acquired files without
publishing those source bytes. Bibliographic provenance may still name the
work, translator, year, edition, holding, and file-derived research result.
When an exact source has affirmative public-domain or reusable-license
evidence, that object may take the corresponding public route after review;
it must not remain closed merely by habit.

## Next source-grounded move

The next useful acquisition target is not another modern reprint. It is the
missing portion of the exact 1899 RNL copy, an institution-controlled 1898
journal supplement/offprint, or an exact 1900 Item with:

1. originating catalog identity and shelfmark;
2. complete page-level return;
3. lawful local research access and file fixity;
4. source-specific rights and redistribution evidence;
5. enough text to compare the 1898, 1899, and 1900 states without trusting
   labels or pagination.

The 1899 route now has a public-safe RNL/RuNEB complete-copy request, but it is
`draft-not-sent`, has no human send approval, and requests no redistribution,
derivative publication, or server processing. If no open object is found for
the other gaps, prepare the same human-approved, rights-aware route to RNL,
RSL, or another identified holding institution. Until then, ToS preserves the
discovered boundaries and the missing edges. No validator may turn this deliberately
incomplete history green by inventing the absent text.
