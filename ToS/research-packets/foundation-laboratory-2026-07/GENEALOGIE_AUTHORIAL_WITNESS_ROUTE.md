## Result

The second work in the frozen cross-work target frame now has an exact
documentary route:

```text
bounded preparatory/title regions and dated correspondence
  -> print manuscript D 20a: title, preface, Treatises I-II
  -> print manuscript D 20b: Treatise III and contents
  -> partial correction-sheet copy K 11 / HAAB C 4616
  -> Naumann 1887 first print E 40
  -> unresolved C 4620 / C 4621 / unnamed hand-copy layer
  -> Naumann 1892 second edition, represented by the existing local Item
  -> Colli/Montinari critical addresses
```

The arrows are routes for comparison, not a claim that one stable or ideal
text moved unchanged through every stage. D 20 records writing and
finalization together. C 4616 preserves only two print sheets plus a
handwritten leaf. Nietzsche, Louise Röder-Wiederhold, and Heinrich
Köselitz/Peter Gast have different responsibilities. The first print, later
copies, the second edition, and a critical text answer different questions.

The University of Basel digital edition is a scholarly digital representation
of D 20, K 11, and E 40. It is not another historical witness and is not
registered as a local ToS Item. Its source-surface indexes supply unusually
strong future address crosswalks without authorizing ToS to copy its text.

No remote source body, image, TEI/XML, OCR, accepted reading, semantic
annotation, translation candidate, new Item, publication route, or recurring
human queue is created by this research.

## Research order

The route was checked in the required order:

## 1. Official archive layer

### D 20a and D 20b

The Goethe- und Schiller-Archiv preserves the print manuscript as two
physical archive objects sharing Mette siglum D 20:

| Layer | Current official identity | Content and responsibility | Digital route |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D 20a | `GSA 71/27,1`; ORES `75094`; DFGA `D-20a`; Kalliope `DE-2060-BE-71-75094` | title, preface, Treatise I, Treatise II; 41 leaves; chiefly Nietzsche, with corrections by Louise Röder-Wiederhold and Peter Gast | [82-canvas IIIF manifest](https://ores.klassik-stiftung.de/ords/rest_api/iiif/digi_gsa/75094/manifest) |
| D 20b | `GSA 71/27,2`; ORES `75097`; DFGA `D-20b`; Kalliope `DE-2060-BE-71-75097` | Treatise III and contents; 70 leaves; Nietzsche, with corrections by Peter Gast | [141-canvas IIIF manifest](https://ores.klassik-stiftung.de/ords/rest_api/iiif/digi_gsa/75097/manifest) |

Both ORES records classify the objects under
`Nietzsche, Friedrich > Werke > Druckmanuskripte` and cite KSA 14,
pp. 377-382. Their exact manifests label the digitized objects `Public
Domain`. That label is object evidence, not a merger with the separate GSA
rules for reproduction and publication.

The split must remain visible. D 20a and D 20b are not two versions of the
whole work, and neither archive leaf count maps automatically one-to-one to
IIIF canvases, Basel source surfaces, printed pages, or critical addresses.

The Basel project further reports that *Genealogie*, unlike Nietzsche's usual

finalized rather than from a normal stock of earlier preparations. That

every earlier or later note irrelevant.

## 2. Official correction-sheet layer

HAAB shelfmark `C 4616` is the surviving correction-sheet copy:

| Field | Current official evidence |

| title | `Zur Genealogie der Moral [Korrekturbogenexemplar]` |
| shelfmark | `C 4616` |
| PPN / EPN | `307154114` / `1649471971` |
| edition | Leipzig, C. G. Naumann, 1887 |
| extent | XIV pages, one leaf, 182 pages, one leaf |
| surviving correction scope | pages 1-16 and 145-160, corresponding to sheets 1 and 10; sheet 1 duplicated; one handwritten leaf |
| provenance and marks | Nietzsche as former owner; correction and marginalia evidence; additional unidentified marks |
| digital route | [55-canvas IIIF manifest](https://haab-digital.klassik-stiftung.de/viewer/api/v1/records/1649471971/manifest/) |

The IIIF manifest exposes the literal placeholder `iiif_license`. The
underlying METS/MODS record instead states `InC 1.0` and `Alle Rechte
vorbehalten`. ToS preserves that asymmetry and infers no reuse permission from
the viewer.

The 2019 historical-critical commentary reports that Nietzsche's and
Köselitz's corrections in C 4616 were executed in the first print, correcting
an older KSA statement that the corrections had not survived. C 4616 is still
partial: absence of a correction there cannot prove absence from missing
sheets or other print-process evidence.

## 3. Basel documentary edition

The University of Basel project
[*Im Schreib-Druck*](https://germanistik.philhist.unibas.ch/de/forschung/forschungsprojekte/ndl/im-schreib-druck/)
edits the print manuscript and associated corrections as document types and
studies their role in textual finalization. Its
[digital edition](https://nietzsche.philhist.unibas.ch/gm/) exposes three
separate objects:

| Basel object | Historical layer | ToS posture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `D20.xml` | print manuscript D 20a/D 20b | remote scholarly representation; no source body admitted |
| `K11.xml` | correction sheets / C 4616 route | remote scholarly representation; partial witness retained |
| `E40.xml` | Naumann 1887 first print | remote edition object, not the 1892 local Item |

The public registry
[`data.json`](https://nietzsche.philhist.unibas.ch/resources/data/data.json)
also lists an appendix object for correspondence and genesis documents. It is
used only as a current metadata route; none of those XML bodies was fetched or
stored.

The D 20
[source-surface index](https://nietzsche.philhist.unibas.ch/dm/index.html)
maps `a1r` onward to title, preface, Treatises I-II and `b1r` onward to
Treatise III. It also keeps additions and corrections on their material
surfaces—for example the pre-stages on `a29v`, a correction to print sheet 8
on `b19r`, and corrections to sheet 12 on `b64r`. Separate indexes expose
[K 11](https://nietzsche.philhist.unibas.ch/cb/index.html),
[E 40](https://nietzsche.philhist.unibas.ch/ed/index.html), the
[appendix and correspondence](https://nietzsche.philhist.unibas.ch/appendix/index.html),
the [timeline](https://nietzsche.philhist.unibas.ch/timeline/index.html), and
the [concordance and pre-stage index](https://nietzsche.philhist.unibas.ch/qv/index.html).

This is the strongest current route for future stable source-surface-to-section
crosswalks. It does not establish that every displayed text is reusable.
The edition became freely viewable in December 2025, but no explicit reusable
content license was found on the inspected edition pages. Open viewing is not
permission to publish, redistribute, or silently ingest its TEI.

## 4. First print, later copies, and second edition

Basel identifies E 40 as the 1887 first print and preserves its own structural
index: an eight-section preface, Treatise I with 17 sections, Treatise II with
25, and Treatise III with 28.

The existing ToS Item is not E 40. It is a fixity-verified scan of the 1892
second Naumann edition:

`tos.item.friedrich-nietzsche.zur-genealogie-der-moral.de-naumann-1892-second.wikimedia-commons-unc-scan-pdf`

It remains useful as later historical print evidence while staying distinct
from the first print, accepted German, and critical text.

The historical-critical commentary also distinguishes:

- `C 4620`, Nietzsche's library copy, digitally viewable but reported without
  Nietzsche reading traces;
- `C 4621`, catalogued without entries but reported missing as of 2018;
- an unnamed `Handexemplar` cited by Montinari without a settled shelfmark,
  possibly but not demonstrably C 4621.

These uncertainties remain claims, not synthetic Items. ToS creates no
phantom “hand copy” to make the route look complete.

## 5. Established genetic and commentary baseline

Andreas Urs Sommer's 2019
[*Kommentar zu Nietzsches Zur Genealogie der Moral*](https://doi.org/10.11588/diglit.70912)
is the historical-critical control for D 20, C 4616, print production,
responsibility, chronology, and the unresolved copy layer. It dates the first
recoverable traces no earlier than mid-July 1887 and the dispatch of the final
part of Treatise III to 28 August. The commentary is scholarly evidence, not
a reusable ToS source body.

William A. B. Parkhurst's 2022
[*A Genetic Interpretation of the Preface of The Genealogy of Morals*](https://doi.org/10.3390/genealogy6040081)
is the leading direct open genetic study of one bounded content question. It
distinguishes preliminary stages, fair copy, print manuscript, correction
sheets, a hand-copy layer, first edition, and later development while using
the changing preface to make an interpretive argument.

That study shows the value of comparing documentary stages. Its terminology
does not override official archive identities, and its interpretation is not
imported as semantic truth.

## 6. Freshest directly relevant work

Joachim Jung and Mirella Carbone's 2024
[*Fünf noch unveröffentlichte Briefe Friedrich Nietzsches*](https://doi.org/10.1515/nietzstu-2024-0018)
publishes newly found correspondence under CC BY 4.0, including Nietzsche's
letter to Louise Röder-Wiederhold of 3 August 1887. The letter strengthens the
chronology and responsibility context; it is not a new D 20 witness.

Beat Röllin's 2024
[*Chronologie der Manuskripte 1885–89. Nachtrag zu KGW IX*](https://doi.org/10.1515/nietzstu-2024-0001)
is the current chronology control, especially for mixed W II 1 layers. It
does not authorize whole-notebook attribution.

The 2024 Basel workshop
[*Im Schreib-Druck. Wie man (nicht) aufhört zu schreiben*](https://germanistik.philhist.unibas.ch/fileadmin/user_upload/germanistik/Schreib-Druck-Workshop-Oktober-2024.pdf)
states the relevant editorial method directly: study documentary genesis,
process, materiality, and end stages without presupposing or manufacturing an
ideal final text.

The digital edition reached provisional completion and first became freely
viewable in December 2025. It is the freshest directly relevant philological
result and supersedes older route descriptions where it supplies more exact
document-surface evidence.

Alexander Prescott-Couch's 2025
[*Nietzschean Genealogy and Philosophical Methodology*](https://doi.org/10.1093/arisoc/aoaf006)
and its 2026 correction concern genealogy as a philosophical method, not the
identity or sequence of D 20, K 11, or E 40. They remain semantic-method leads
outside this textual-witness route.
