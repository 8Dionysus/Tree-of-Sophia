## Result

The third work in the frozen cross-work target frame now has a documentary
route stronger than the already acquired 1906 archive-edition witness:

```text
region-only preparatory and project evidence
  -> D 22 / GSA 71/29 print manuscript
       |-> A 311 Overbeck copy, early 1889
       `-> Koegel/Naumann 1895 first print
             -> later Nietzsche-Archiv editions
                  -> existing 1906 aggregate Item
                       -> Schlechta 1956 de-suppressed text
                            -> Colli/Montinari KGW VI 3 (1969)
                                 -> eKGWB stable AC addresses
```

This is not a single linear transmission of one immutable text. The print
manuscript has two title states, nontrivial leaf order, corrections attributed
by established scholarship to Heinrich Köselitz, and one leaf whose reverse
belongs to an earlier *Ecce homo* / *Der Fall Wagner* state. The Overbeck copy
is an independent near-author copy, not Nietzsche's autograph or an
author-approved print. The 1895 first print silently incorporates editorial
corrections and suppresses or changes four passages. The 1906 Item is a later
archive-edition witness, not evidence that those interventions were repaired.

The proposed `Gesetz wider das Christenthum` ending is a separate associated
route:

```text
struck printer instruction after AC 62 in D 22
  -> GSA 71/32, fol. 47: "Gegeben am Tage des Heils ..."
       -> Colli/Montinari editorial placement after AC 62
```

The leaf is physically stored inside the D 25 / *Ecce homo* archive object,
does not itself carry the title `Gesetz wider das Christenthum`, and is absent
from both D 22's surviving text body and the Overbeck copy. Its physical
location does not make it part of *Ecce homo*, while the printer instruction,
adhesive evidence, related drafts, and Nietzsche's correspondence make an
association with *Der Antichrist* philologically strong. ToS therefore records
it as a contested associated adjunct, never as `AC 63`, a silent appendix, or
a whole-work equivalence.

No remote source body, image, manuscript transcription, OCR, critical-edition
body, accepted reading, semantic annotation, translation candidate, new Item,
publication route, or recurring human queue is created by this research.

## 1. Official archive layer

### D 22 is the print manuscript, not a frictionless original

The Goethe- und Schiller-Archiv currently identifies the print manuscript as:

| GSA | `71/29` |
| ORES | `75100` |
| Kalliope | `DE-2060-BE-71-75100` |
| Mette / DFGA | `D 22` / `D-22` |
| archive title | `Der Antichrist. Fluch auf das Christenthum.` |
| extent / hand | 51 leaves / `egh` |
| publication relation | print manuscript for Koegel's Naumann 1895 edition |
| digital route | [104-canvas IIIF manifest](https://ores.klassik-stiftung.de/ords/rest_api/iiif/digi_gsa/75100/manifest) |

The exact ORES record supplies two material warnings:

- leaf 13 precedes leaf 12, and leaf 35 precedes leaf 34 as a folded sheet;
- leaf 2r is the original *Antichrist* title leaf, while leaf 51v is a
  preliminary state of an earlier *Ecce homo. Der Fall Wagner* 2–4.

The archive object therefore cannot be normalized silently into a simple
1-to-51 reading order or assigned wholesale to one work on both sides of every
leaf. Its IIIF manifest labels the exact digital object `Public Domain`.
That object-level label remains distinct from the GSA's general reproduction
and publication rules.

The official record calls the object autograph. The stronger statement that
the manuscript also contains some Köselitz corrections comes from the
historical-critical commentary, not from the inspected ORES hand field. ToS
keeps those responsibility sources distinct.

### Preparatory notebooks are region-only soil

HAdW's established production history identifies `W II 1`, `W II 2`, and
`W II 3` as the notebooks behind Nietzsche's February 1888 report that the
first written attempt at his revaluation project was complete. That does not
make any of the three a whole-work draft of *Der Antichrist*. At that point the
project, scope, order, and title still differed materially.

Current GSA/Kalliope identities preserve the notebook family:

| `W II 6` / `W-II-6` | `71/162` / `75271` | 1888; 76 leaves | mixed revaluation, *Fall Wagner*, *Götzen-Dämmerung*, and *Ecce* material |

The University of Basel's completed
[*Der späte Nietzsche*](https://germanistik.philhist.unibas.ch/de/forschung/forschungsprojekte/ndl/der-spaete-nietzsche/)
project places W II 1–2 in KGW IX 6, W II 3–4 in KGW IX 7, W II 5 in
KGW IX 8, and W II 6–7 in KGW IX 9. Its differentiated transcription follows
the spatial order of the inscriptions and preserves deletions, overwriting,
revision, and draft character. The project explicitly rejects reducing the
notebooks to detached, smoothed reading texts.

The strongest currently located exact region claims are narrower:

- W II 4 contains later preliminary states for AC 28, 29, 30, and 36;
- the reverse-running end/beginning region of W II 3 contains preliminary
  `Wir Hyperboreer` material, Renan excerpts, and Christianity notes;
- HAdW maps individual AC passages to exact W II 3, W II 5, and W II 6
  locations.

Those links support future leaf/region-to-section comparisons. They do not
authorize a whole-notebook membership edge, one reconstructed ideal text, or
an assumption that every note about Christianity belongs to *Der Antichrist*.

The exact current archive container is:

HAdW's exact
[commentary route](https://doi.org/10.11588/diglit.70914#0339)
reports that the leaf begins `Gegeben am Tage des Heils ...`; the title
`Gesetz wider das Christenthum` is instead preserved in a struck printer
instruction after AC 62. Adhesive traces, continuous foliation, a Köselitz
note, and Nietzsche's early-December 1888 letter to Georg Brandes support the
association. The reverse carries *Ecce homo* preliminary material, and nearby
leaves introduce a further `Der Hammer redet` / *Zarathustra* question.

This is exactly the sort of topology a foundation must preserve: physical
container, leaf side, proposed work association, and later editorial placement
are four different facts.

A 311 is valuable precisely because it is not another label for D 22. It is a
copy by Overbeck, has its own pagination and material history, and omits the

readings and order, but not as Nietzsche's autograph, an author-approved
edition, or an automatic correction of D 22.

## 3. The 1895 first print is an editorial witness

The exact physical scan still has a source-quality gap. The current
bibliographic lead is Google Books volume `kd8cDQEACAAJ`, associated with
Cornell key `CORNELL:31924024495412`; it currently exposes metadata but no
view or download. No official open 1895 scan with adequate item provenance was
found in the dated search. An unprovenanced Internet Archive upload labelled
1895 remains rejected.

The first print's identity and intervention history are nevertheless
established:

- Koegel published *Der Antichrist* in volume VIII of the
  Großoktavausgabe, C. G. Naumann, with imprint year 1895;
- the rejected older subtitle `Versuch einer Kritik des Christenthums` was
  restored;
- Köselitz corrections were incorporated silently;
- four passages that Elisabeth Förster-Nietzsche found objectionable were
  suppressed or altered, affecting current KSA 6 locations 200,14f.;
  207,32–208,3; 211,6; and 253,16–20;
- those interventions propagated through later Nietzsche-Archiv editions.

The existing 1906 aggregate Item is consequently a historically exact local
witness but not a presumed repair. Passage-level comparison must prove any
difference from the 1895 state; edition date alone proves nothing.

Karl Schlechta published a text without the four distortions in 1956. HAdW
identifies Colli and Montinari's KGW VI 3 (1969) as the first reliable text.
These are later editorial-critical achievements, not recovered author-approved
prints.

## 5. Established and fresh research

### Established baseline

Andreas Urs Sommer's 2013 HAdW
[overview](https://doi.org/10.11588/diglit.70914#0031) is the principal
historical-critical control for:

- D 22's two title pages and Köselitz corrections;
- Overbeck A 311;
- the 1895 editorial interventions and their later propagation;
- Schlechta 1956 and Colli/Montinari 1969;
- the separate, materially complicated `Gesetz` question.

The commentary is in copyright and is used only as scholarship. Its OCR is
not a source witness for Nietzsche's text.

### Fresh direct genesis work

Thomas H. Brobjer's chapter
[*The Origin and Development of The Antichrist: In Nietzsche's
Workshop*](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-06550-6_4), first online
2025-11-04, studies roughly 300 pages of winter 1887/88 notes and their later
revision. It argues that most of *Der Antichrist* can be constructed from that
material. The chapter is subscription-only; only publisher metadata, abstract,
and notes were inspected.

For ToS this is a strong experiment hypothesis and a warning at once:

- it motivates exact note-region-to-AC-section comparison;
- it identifies W II 3 and W II 4 regions that older reading-text editions
  hide;
- its claim that most contents can be constructed is an interpretation, not
  whole-notebook membership, textual identity, or a recovered missing work.

Giorgio Di Sarno's
[2025 review](https://doi.org/10.1515/nietzstu-2025-0007) supplies a useful
method counterweight. It warns against turning inferred hidden intentions or
detached Nachlass material into the controlling meaning of a published work,
and stresses textual form, narrative instability, and research context.
That supports a comparison design in which genetic evidence and work-level
reading remain separate outputs.

### 2026 relevance check

Claus Zittel's in-press
[*Literarische Spiegelungen*](https://hdl.handle.net/10278/5117127), registered
for *Nietzscheforschung* 33 (2026), reconstructs Manu/Hugo transmission and
literary self-reflection. It is a relevant future source/semantic lead, but the
institutional record currently carries no file and reserves rights. It changes
neither D 22 nor the editorial witness chain.

No located 2026 philological result supersedes the official archive identities
or HAdW's transmission baseline. Freshness therefore sharpens future semantic
and intertextual questions without rewriting the physical route.

## 6. Foundation consequences for ToS

### Stable enough to index now

ToS may index, as metadata:

- work identity;
- archive, library, edition, and digital-object identifiers;
- document type and physical extent;
- exact leaf-side or region scope when a source supports it;
- responsibility and intervention claims with their own source;
- declared rights at the exact object or platform layer;
- uncertainty, contradiction, and unresolved transport;
- non-equivalence between manuscript, copy, print, later edition, and critical
  address.

### Not stable enough to promote

This route does not justify:

- a whole-notebook *Antichrist* membership assertion;
- an immutable or author-final source text;
- silent rectification of D 22's leaf order;
- treatment of A 311 as Nietzsche's autograph;
- treatment of 1895 or 1906 as accepted German or the critical text;
- appending the `Gesetz` as `AC 63`;
- treating a physical archive container as proof of work membership;
- a source/translation alignment;
- a sign, lemma, concept, semantic relation, or graph-canonical edge;
- a new transfer sample, target gold, human task, or publication right.

### First defensible future experiment

The strongest later laboratory question is not “which text is correct?” but a
small, versioned comparison with one exact locus:

1. select one AC passage affected by a known editorial intervention, or one
   exact W II 3/W II 4 preliminary region;
2. freeze the exact source surfaces and rights posture before looking at
   outputs;
3. compare D 22, A 311 where applicable, 1895/1906, and a critical address as
   distinct witnesses;
4. require independent diplomatic readings and German-assisted review;
5. measure what each route preserves, loses, normalizes, or invents;
6. keep semantic interpretation downstream from witnessed differences.

This is outside the frozen golden-kernel transfer plan. The documentary stages
are deliberately named rather than called A/B/C, because A/B/C already means
**A = no kernel**, **B = method only**, and **C = reviewed Zarathustra kernel**
in that experiment.
