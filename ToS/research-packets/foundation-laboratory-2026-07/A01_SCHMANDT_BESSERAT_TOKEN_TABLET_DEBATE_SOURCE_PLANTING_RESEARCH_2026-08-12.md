# A01 — Schmandt-Besserat and the token-to-tablet debate

Date: 2026-08-12
Status: metadata-only source planting; model-authored; unreviewed
Decision: plant Schmandt-Besserat's direct theory Work as a necessary classical
source, but do not promote `token→tablet` as one accepted historical or
semantic edge. Preserve the theory, its empirical catalog, its critiques, and
the strongest current alternatives as separately returnable evidence.

## Why this source matters

The A01 backlog asks for the historiography of tokens and the
Schmandt-Besserat debate because the incoming route from material accounting
objects to tablets cannot be represented responsibly by a single arrow. Denise
Schmandt-Besserat's *Before Writing, Vol. I: From Counting to Cuneiform* is the
direct classical statement of the theory. Its companion Volume II is the
catalog of the artifacts on which the theory was built. Both are indispensable
research sources even where later work rejects their classification or causal
sequence.

Planting the theory does not settle it. A physical clay object, its geometric
form, its archaeological context, an inferred accounting function, an
impression, a tablet mark, a sign identity, a numerical or commodity value,
and a historical-continuity claim are different things. ToS must retain the
distance between them.

## 1. Official and classical documentation

### Exact Work and companion-data boundary

The [BiblioVault record](https://bibliovault.org/BV.book.epl?ISBN=9780292707832)
identifies *Before Writing, Vol. I: From Counting to Cuneiform*, Denise
Schmandt-Besserat, University of Texas Press, 1992. It records cloth ISBN
`978-0-292-70783-2`, current paper ISBN `978-1-4773-2576-6`, and Library of
Congress class `CJ4867.S36 1992`. The current [University of Texas Press Volume
I page](https://utpress.utexas.edu/9781477325766/) describes the 2022 paperback
issue. These manifestations do not require duplicate Works.

The publisher separately identifies [*Before Writing, Vol. II: A Catalog of
Near Eastern Tokens*](https://utpress.utexas.edu/9780292726703/), ISBN
`978-0-292-72670-3`, as the 1992 catalog of several thousand artifacts. Its
fields include country, site, type and subtype, chronology, stratigraphy,
museum, accession or field number, prior publication, material, size,
drawings, and photographs. Volume I is the interpretive theory Work; Volume II
is the primary-data layer that must be consulted before individual artifact or
token-to-sign claims can be tested. This slice models only Volume I and does
not collapse the two volumes.

The publisher's [*How Writing Came About*
page](https://utpress.utexas.edu/9780292777040/) confirms that the two 1992
volumes set out a theory based on a selection of about 8,000 proposed tokens or
counters from 116 sites. The later book is a condensed presentation and is not
a substitute for the monographs.

### The thesis in its strongest form

Schmandt-Besserat's author-maintained University of Texas site presents the
[evolution-of-writing thesis](https://sites.utexas.edu/dsb/tokens/the-evolution-of-writing/)
as a direct sequence: clay objects interpreted as counters represented units
of goods; impressions on envelopes externalized their contents; impressed
tablets replaced envelopes and tokens; and pictographic signs followed. It
also asserts semantic identity between selected tokens, impressions, and
later marks.

This route is authoritative for what the author claims, not neutral authority
for the truth of the whole sequence. The site's strong continuity language
must remain attributed to Schmandt-Besserat and tested at the exact artifact,
context, mark, and sign levels.

### Classical criticism and epistemological control

Stephen Lieberman's 1980 article [*Of Clay Pebbles, Hollow Clay Balls, and
Writing: A Sumerian View*](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/abs/10.2307/504711),
DOI `10.2307/504711`, is an early direct criticism. Its publisher abstract
contests the chronological and geographical justification for the proposed
lineage, the precision of formal comparisons, the explanatory adequacy of the
sequence, and the classification of heterogeneous clay objects. The article
is retained as a bibliographic and methodological control; no body is
acquired.

The debate was subsequently recorded in Robert Englund's 1993 review [*The
origins of script*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17810210/), DOI
`10.1126/science.260.5114.1670`, Paul Zimansky's 1993 review of *Before
Writing* (DOI `10.1179/jfa.1993.20.4.513`, JSTOR `530080`), and Piotr
Michalowski's 1993 review *Tokenism* in *American Anthropologist* 95,
996–999. These records establish a sustained scholarly dispute; metadata does
not authorize acquisition or make any one review dispositive.

Peter Damerow's open CDLI article [*The Origins of Writing as a Problem of
Historical Epistemology*](https://cdli.earth/articles/cdlj/2006-1) supplies a
broader classical control. Proto-writing may represent and transmit knowledge
without representing oral language. Its emergence need not be a one-dimensional
progression: relatively independent processes can interact, substitute for
one another, reorganize, and become integrated. This makes an attractive
linear narrative an object of inquiry rather than a default data model.

## 2. Established top scholarship

Lucy E. Bennison-Chapman's 2019 article [*Reconsidering ‘Tokens’: The
Neolithic Origins of Accounting or Multifunctional, Utilitarian
Tools?*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/cambridge-archaeological-journal/article/abs/reconsidering-tokens-the-neolithic-origins-of-accounting-or-multifunctional-utilitarian-tools/7E6C04CB040AD8AA0EA84B94D4D275C4),
DOI `10.1017/S0959774318000513`, is the strongest established empirical
reassessment found. It examines almost 3,000 objects from 56 sites and rejects
their treatment as one uniform Neolithic symbolic or accounting system.
Counting remains one possible function among multiple utilitarian and social
uses. The category `token` is therefore often an interpretation applied to a
material object, not a directly observed object type.

Her 2023 article [*Tools of the Trade: Accounting Tokens as an Alternative to
Text in the Cuneiform
World*](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/727776), DOI
`10.1086/727776`, directly tests the replacement narrative. Clay objects
interpreted as administrative tokens persisted alongside tags, bullae, seals,
sealings, and written texts into the first millennium BCE. Coexistence
falsifies a simple claim that writing universally replaced tokens. It does not
by itself falsify every local influence, impression, or continuity hypothesis.

These two studies change the base unit of inquiry. ToS must begin with an
identified object and find context, then record competing functional
classifications. It must not begin with a preaccepted universal token class and
infer the object's purpose from its shape.

## 3. Fresh current relevance check

### Multiple media and context-supported comparison

Kelley, Cartolano, and Ferrara's [*Seals and signs: tracing the origins of
writing in ancient South-west
Asia*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/seals-and-signs-tracing-the-origins-of-writing-in-ancient-southwest-asia/B3C2D400F3F80A7A0162D9035C9C2804),
DOI `10.15184/aqy.2024.165`, was published online in 2024 and in *Antiquity*
99(403) in February 2025 under CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. It finds plausible
context-supported relationships between some seal motifs and proto-cuneiform
signs while warning that shape resemblance alone has no sound method and that
`symbol-hopping` is difficult to establish. Seals and writing can share forms
and semantic associations while remaining distinct information systems.

The consequence is not to replace a token-only origin with a seal-only origin.
The candidate media set must remain open: tokens, bullae, tags, seals,
sealings, numerical tablets, containers, and other administrative practices
may contribute different operations and meanings.

### Episodic and regional information transitions

Matthews, Richardson, Fazeli Nashli, and de Groene's 2025 article [*Information
revolutions and information transitions: counting, sealing, writing in Iran
10,000–300 BC*](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/122518/), DOI
`10.1515/janeh-2024-0027`, is available from the University of Reading record
under CC BY 4.0. Its long-duration synthesis treats counting, sealing, and
writing as episodic and regional trajectories. Systems are integrated in some
places, detached in others; writing may disappear while sealing continues.
This evidence is incompatible with one universal irreversible ladder.

### Historiography remains an active method problem

Massimo Maiocchi's 2025 [*A Critical Examination of the Current Paradigms on
the Origin of Writing in
Mesopotamia*](https://riviste.unimi.it/index.php/enkiandptah/article/view/27180),
DOI `10.54103/eap/27180`, is CC BY-SA 4.0. It finds continuing lack of
consensus and warns that disciplinary and civilizational prestige can shape
origin narratives. It argues for an interconnected technological, social, and
cultural account rather than a single privileged invention path.

Targeted searches for direct 2026 scholarship on clay tokens,
Schmandt-Besserat, and the origin of proto-cuneiform found no stronger
peer-reviewed correction than these direct 2025 studies as of 2026-08-12.
Freshness is not treated as quality: generic popular summaries, repository
uploads without a rights route, and technical cuneiform NLP papers that do not
test the historical transition were rejected.

## 4. Foundation model for ToS

The notation `token→tablet` is useful only as a navigation label. It must
expand into independently evidenced candidate relations:

1. morphological resemblance between an object, impression, mark, or sign;
2. chronological succession, overlap, or absence;
3. archaeological association and exact find context;
4. operational transfer, such as an object making an impression;
5. proposed semantic equivalence;
6. proposed functional continuity;
7. proposed replacement of one practice by another;
8. coexistence or parallel use;
9. influence from another medium, especially seals and sealings;
10. integration, substitution, reorganization, or local abandonment.

The minimum evidence topology distinguishes:

- `clay_object` from its proposed role as `accounting_token`;
- observed geometric form from a type assignment;
- object identifier, museum or field number, custody, and find context;
- chronology and stratigraphy, including uncertainty and later revision;
- material, dimensions, manufacturing traces, wear, perforation, and use
  traces;
- container or bulla membership and sealing relation;
- exact physical impression and the object proposed to have produced it;
- tablet mark, grapheme occurrence, normalized sign identity, and sign-list
  coordinate;
- numerical value, metrological system, commodity value, and contextual
  reading;
- artifact function, communication function, and administrative practice;
- historical continuity hypothesis, its author, version, evidence, critics,
  counterexamples, and alternatives.

### Pair-level comparison contract

Every proposed object-to-mark or object-to-sign correspondence must preserve:

1. exact anchors for both compared things;
2. image or drawing provenance and rights at each side;
3. date and find context for both sides;
4. comparison transformation: view, scale, rotation, reflection, tracing, and
   normalization;
5. whether contact or impression is physically demonstrated;
6. evidence for numerical, commodity, semantic, or functional identity;
7. negative cases and morphologically similar alternatives;
8. competing media and causal hypotheses;
9. maker, method, software or model version, and review state.

Shape alone cannot establish object identity, shared meaning, function, or
historical ancestry. Numerical correspondences may be stronger than proposed
commodity-sign correspondences, but their validity remains scoped to an exact
system, period, place, and evidence set.

### Negative invariants

- A geometric clay object is not automatically an accounting token.
- A shape match is not a semantic match.
- A semantic match is not proof of direct descent.
- Chronological succession is not causation.
- Co-occurrence is not replacement.
- Survival after writing refutes simple universal replacement, not every local
  continuity hypothesis.
- A publisher summary of a theory is not primary archaeological evidence.
- A current critique does not erase the historical theory or its catalog.

## 5. Laboratory design

No experiment is run in this slice. The prepared A/B/C design is:

### A — classical-theory extraction

Extract every transition claim from Volume I with exact page anchors. Separate
observed object data, attributed classifications, morphometric comparison,
semantic assignment, functional inference, and historical narrative. Preserve
the author's qualifiers and distinguish evidence described in Volume I from
catalog records supplied by Volume II.

### B — exact pair adjudication

Sample proposed object-to-impression and object-to-sign pairs. Return to the
Volume II catalog, current museum or excavation record, exact tablet or bulla,
current sign coordinate, and critical literature. Test morphology under
explicit transformations, chronology, find context, physical producibility,
value, and counterexamples. Include negative controls with close shape but
incompatible context or function.

### C — competing multi-source reconstruction

Allow rule-based, statistical, vision, or LLM-assisted methods to propose a
multi-media transition graph. Require every edge to declare its relation type,
source anchors, uncertainty, alternatives, and whether it represents
observation, source-reported interpretation, or new reconstruction. Test
token, bulla, seal, tag, numerical-tablet, and regional trajectories without
forcing one global sequence.

Compare the arms by:

- false morphological links and unsupported semantic mappings;
- category errors between object, function, mark, sign, and meaning;
- preservation of counterexamples and competing hypotheses;
- source returnability and provenance completeness;
- calibration of uncertainty and abstention;
- time, compute, financial cost, and human attention per adjudicated pair;
- error visibility when an identifier, dating, image, or sign-list mapping
  changes.

Local software, vision, OCR, retrieval, and LLM trials belong in the
`abyss-stack` laboratory. ToS owns the source identities, evidence topology,
input and output anchors, and eventual reviewed claims. Human review is
reserved for consequential unresolved correspondences and sampled controls,
not routine retyping or approval of every machine step.

## Rights and access route

The publisher pages currently mark both monographs as all-rights-reserved
books. Their public metadata and descriptions are sufficient for this planting
but not evidence of permission to ingest the book bodies. University of Texas
Press directs book permission questions to its permissions workflow and gives
`permissions@utpress.utexas.edu` when the Copyright Clearance Center cannot
grant a requested use.

A public-safe draft asks for access-controlled, local, noncommercial
solo-plus-AI research use of both volumes: OCR or transcription, local
indexing, embeddings, source-grounded comparison, and bounded quotation. It
does not request model training, public source-file hosting, derivative
publication, server processing, or redistribution. The draft is not sent and
must be refreshed and explicitly approved before any communication.
