material, and inferential limits remain visible.

The source's useful foundation lesson is not a bare `vertical` or `horizontal`
label. A future ToS observation must keep at least these coordinates distinct:

1. the physical top and sides of the artifact;
2. the attachment axis inferred from holes, cord channels, or impressions;
3. the direction and sequence of lines or columns;
4. the intrinsic orientation of individual signs;
5. the reader's proposed traversal order;
6. any rotation or normalization applied by a drawing, photograph, edition,
   transcription, viewer, or machine-learning input pipeline.

Collapsing them would turn a modern display convention into a false ancient
fact.

### The classical dispute, not a single inherited fact

Fitzgerald enters a long-running argument rather than beginning from
consensus. Her compressed historiography distinguishes several positions:

- the early rotation theory associated with Delitzsch and supported in
  different forms by Deimel and Falkenstein;
- Marvin A. Powell's 1981 argument that cuneiform remained vertically read
  through roughly 2300 BCE and that stylus manipulation helped motivate the
  transition;
- S. A. Picchioni's 1980 and 1984–1985 arguments from orientation-bearing
  objects for a much later change;
- Albrecht Goetze's 1950 observation that the suspension holes of 47
  Sīn-iddinam tags imply columnar rather than modern line orientation.

The [originating Visible Language record](https://journals.uc.edu/index.php/vl/article/view/5328)
for Powell's “Three Problems in the History of Cuneiform Writing: Origins,
Direction of Script, and Literacy” confirms the 1981 publication and its
vertical-through-c.2300/stylus-mechanism thesis. Fitzgerald instead follows
Picchioni toward a change during or after the Kassite period. These dates are
competing scholarly models and must never be flattened into one chronology.

### Fitzgerald's exact material argument

The two-page article makes several separable moves:

1. Modern editions conventionally present even early cuneiform as lines read
   left to right, although pictographic forms appear rotated under that view.
2. Objects with an independently recoverable physical orientation—vases,
   stelae, statues, cylinder seals, and attached tags—can constrain ancient
   reading direction more strongly than normalized publication layout.
3. Ur III `pisan dub-ba` tags commonly have two connected holes used to attach
   them to containers of tablets or goods.
4. Fitzgerald names Institut Catholique de Paris objects ICP 1, 2, 446, and
   447 as especially clear cases: the holes appear at the left under modern
   horizontal display but at the top under a top-to-bottom, right-to-left
   reading reconstruction.
5. TUT 164-03 is used to show that published drawings may omit holes, making
   photographs and physical morphology indispensable.
6. Goetze's Old Babylonian tag group and the Hammurabi stele/cylinder-seal
   comparison are used as later controls.
7. The suggestion that Kassite innovation or the international Late Bronze
   Age environment caused the change is explicitly prospective, not a result
   demonstrated by the article.

The article therefore supports a material inference route. It does not prove
that every cuneiform object in a period shares one orientation, that sign
rotation and text-direction change occurred simultaneously, or that the
proposed social cause is established.

## 2. Established scholarship and methodological controls

### Pisan-dub-ba corpus and archive function

Richard Carroll Nelson's 1976 University of Minnesota dissertation,
*Pisan-dub-ba Texts from the Sumerian Ur III Dynasty*, remains the foundational
large treatment. Current institutional catalog records describe it as a
440-leaf dissertation. Nelson's inventory and later scholarship make the tags
an archive-organization corpus, not merely convenient samples of handwriting.

The tag's written content, its container function, its suspension mechanism,
and its displayed orientation are different evidence layers. The first line
`pisan-dub-ba` can identify document type; it cannot by itself locate physical
top, prove which side bore a complete cord channel, or establish reading
direction. Conversely, a hole can constrain attachment geometry without
translating the inscription.

- the ATF is a normalized textual representation rather than a geometric
  transcription of the clay surface;
- the catalog's `tag` classification does not encode the attachment axis;
- availability of a photograph does not mean the image may be republished;
- a current artifact ID does not freeze image orientation, crop, lighting,
  metadata, or transcription forever.

A future physical-orientation claim must cite an exact image/object
observation, view, transform, and review event, not merely the artifact page.

### Orientation needs a transform-aware evidence model

The minimum future observation packet should separate:

- `artifact_ref`, physical face, edge, and current source revision;
- observed holes, channels, cord impressions, breaks, and uncertainty;
- image owner, view identifier, crop, rotation, mirroring, and lighting;
- line/column segmentation independent of semantic reading;
- proposed reading order and sign orientation as separately reviewable
  interpretations;
- cited comparative objects and the temporal interval over which an inference
  is allowed;
- reviewer, competing interpretation, confidence, and supersession history.

This should be expressed as source observations and claim packets before any
graph promotion. A direct edge such as `Ur III -> vertical writing` would erase
object variation, source transforms, and the disputed chronology.

## 3. Fresh current relevance check

### Stępień: direct 2025 publication-level confirmation of the tag argument

Marek Stępień's “The New ‘pisan-dub-ba’ Tablet from the Time of the Ur III
Dynasty, in the Collection of the National Museum in Wrocław (Poland)” appears
in nominal volume 14–15 (2023–2024) of *Anabasis*, but the journal records its
actual publication on 27 December 2025. The nominal issue and actual release
date must remain distinct.

The refereed article reports more than 800 known tags, describes the usual two
openings on the left side, explicitly affirms Fitzgerald's observation as
important to the orientation debate, and applies the material logic to a newly
published Wrocław tablet. Its license is CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. It strengthens the
continued relevance of the physical attachment argument; it does not by
itself adjudicate every period, counterexample, or proposed date of the global
script transition.

### Surdi 2026: representation can erase the decisive morphology

Mirko Surdi's publisher-hosted CDLB 2026:1, “Improving Legibility: Visual
Strategies for Four Unpublished Cuneiform Objects from Ghent University,” is
the freshest directly useful methodological control located. It studies Ur III
objects with 2D+ relighting and a `pisan-dub-ba` tag that preserves negative
cord impressions.

The article demonstrates that optimization for sign legibility and
optimization for physical morphology can conflict. A high-contrast `Sketch 1`
view can flatten depth until cord impressions become indistinguishable from
damage; a composite with a shaded layer preserves more morphology. This is a
direct warning for the ToS laboratory:

- OCR-friendly or sign-friendly views cannot be assumed adequate for
  attachment-axis or object-orientation judgments;
- raw photographs, relightable surface data, derived visualizations, line
  art, and transcription are different witnesses;
- every visual transform used by a model or person must be recorded;
- agreement reached only on one processed view is not independent
  corroboration.

The CDLB 2026:1 article is itself CC BY 4.0 except noted material and artifact
images. Its visual examples remain separately governed.

## Laboratory consequences

The smallest honest A/B/C study is transform-aware and source-visible:

- **A — historical-argument reproduction:** encode Fitzgerald's cited
  objects, chronological premises, and inferential steps without deciding the
  outcome; verify that a reader can return from every statement to the exact
  article paragraph and artifact coordinate.
- **B — current physical-evidence review:** inspect the exact current photos
  of `P134765`, `P134766`, `P139616`, `P139619`, and `P135739`; independently
  mark physical edges, holes/channels, cord evidence, proposed hanging axis,
  text axes, uncertainty, and source transforms. Do not expose Fitzgerald's
  conclusion during first-pass marking.
- **C — representation and counterexample stress:** compare raw photographs,
  line art, normalized page views, high-contrast sign views, and morphology-
  preserving composites; add independently oriented monuments and dated
  counterexamples. Measure which conclusions survive view changes and which
  depend on omitted morphology.

Human review belongs only at consequential disagreements: uncertain physical
top, ambiguous cord feature versus damage, conflicting line segmentation, or
promotion of a period-level claim. Routine metadata and repeated obvious cases
remain machine-prepared and source-returnable for a solo+AI workflow.

No A/B/C run is executed in this planting.
