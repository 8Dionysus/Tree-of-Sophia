# Sextus, Outlines: German passages for the Antiquity stand

The 15 places already cited by the stand are transcribed from Eugen
Pappenheim's 1877 translation, *Des Sextus Empiricus pyrrhoneische
Grundzüge*. The Bayerische Staatsbibliothek identifies Pappenheim as
1831–1901 and describes this printing: <https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/details/bsb11413194>.
Its page images are supplied by the [IIIF manifest](https://api.digitale-sammlungen.de/iiif/presentation/v2/bsb11413194/manifest).
The translation was published in 1877 and its translator died in 1901;
the library specifies separate terms for its digital images, which are not
redistributed here.

I checked each German passage against the printed page and its section number:
I.1–4, p. 23; I.6–8, pp. 24–25; I.10–11, pp. 25–26; I.21, p. 29;
I.23–24, pp. 29–30; I.29, p. 31; I.164, p. 62; II.13, pp. 92–93.
The printed sentence is retained along with old spelling, parenthetical
clarifications and punctuation; line-break hyphens are joined. The passage
for I.6 begins after a semicolon, within the sentence begun in I.5. For I.23–24
the print's numeral 24 falls within the list belonging to I.23 in the Greek
sectioning; the German split follows the Greek meaning and keeps the printed
punctuation. II.13 ends at a semicolon as in the Greek sectioning.

This review covers these passages only. The library holds a full scan and a
separate OCR candidate was retained for research, but neither a complete
corrected German reading text nor a full-text corpus intake is asserted here.
