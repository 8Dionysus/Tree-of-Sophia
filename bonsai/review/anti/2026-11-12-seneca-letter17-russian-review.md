# Seneca, letter 17.1: 1884 Russian witness

The first January 1884 issue of *Вѣра и Разумъ*, philosophy section p. 79
(PDF leaf 167), prints the opening of Seneca's letter 17 as its selected
«Письмо II». The scan is available from [Азбука веры](https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/assets/uploads/vera-i-razum/%D0%92%D0%B5%D1%80%D0%B0%20%D0%B8%20%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B7%D1%83%D0%BC.%201884.%20%D0%AF%D0%BD%D0%B2%D0%B0%D1%80%D1%8C.%20%D0%9A%D0%BD.%201.pdf);
the downloaded file has SHA-256
`0ff650e8ae6a99129fe484b91c568d2e99d6d1e71ca2b8efc3bf21d94764b213`.
The publication gives no translator's name. A later editorial note suggests
P. Krasnov for the serial translations but does not establish this particular
translator's identity. The quotation is attributed to the anonymous journal
translation, not to the later, differently worded Krasnov edition. It was
published in 1884 and is treated as public domain on the stated anonymous-work
term bases in Russia, the US and the EU.

The Russian transcription was read against the printed page. It preserves
pre-reform spelling and the printed wording, joins line wraps and normalizes
spacing around punctuation. The continuous Latin of 17.1–2, including the
stand's exact 17.1, was aligned against the corresponding continuous Russian
span with `bonsai/lib/align.mjs`; its sentence pairs were inspected. The
smallest corresponding run of complete sentences renders 17.1 and stops
before 17.2. The retained alignment candidate, including both inputs, has
SHA-256 `9e2c2b577722934ecc1d024d863bcf603dc761105ca4531a219bfca6374f24ba`.
The second Latin section provides context for the boundary and is not added as
another stand place. This is a bounded review, not a complete-letter collation.
