## Translator identity on the published source itself

For William James, *The Principles of Psychology*, XI p403a, Santiago Hamelau's 2024 article identifies him as its author on the title and explicitly says on p7 n15 that its translations are his unless otherwise stated. The source-bound PDF and maker/second page images are registered and both readers inspected the exact page; the Spanish translation has documented semantic qualifications and has not been accepted. `witness-person-source ... translator 'Santiago Hamelau' https://revistas.upb.edu.co/index.php/escritos/es/article/view/8822` rejects the journal publisher because it is not in the fixed trusted catalogue/reference host list. Wikidata's entity search returned no match. Please provide a source-bound participant route for an exact published edition's own title and translator statement, tied to fetched bytes and independent reading; keep unknown birth/death and the separate quotation/rights reviews, and do not accept arbitrary self-attribution without those checks. Gate stays shut in the meantime.

## Different translation editions for separate places

Baumgarten *Aesthetica* English §13 is accepted from Maskarinec/Roberts in Berndt 2020. A separate complete §14 translation by Chris van Rompaey appears in a published secondary PDF https://pdf.blucher.com.br/designproceedings/ped2022/6743994.pdf, PDF p6 n8; the secondary page points to his 2017 paper p4 and his own paper states that the subsequent translations are his. His original paper page has not been inspected and the PDF's 27-word quotation is italic throughout. `witness_conflict` rejects extending the same language with a different edition. Please consider a supported per-place separate edition and source/rights/review route that preserves the existing accepted witness, independent reading and provenance; do not merge attribution or rights between the two. The new candidate remains unaccepted.

## Collective attribution without inventing individual translators

For Schiller, *Über den Grund des Vergnügens an tragischen Gegenständen*, III p150, the six-author article Menninghaus et al., “Towards a Psychological Construct of Being Moved” (PLOS ONE, 2015), https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0128451 explicitly attributes the quoted English sentence to “our translation”; it does not identify which author or authors translated it. Treating each as an individually confirmed translator would overstate the source; calling the translation anonymous would contradict the collective attribution. Please consider a source-bound, independently reviewed collective contributor route that preserves the actual uncertainty, and separate rights and quotation review. The candidate is research only: source-bound page reading and full acceptance remain outstanding.
