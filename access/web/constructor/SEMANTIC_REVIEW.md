# Grounded reading review — 2026-09-12

Subject: the local demonstration revision based on
`98b43f0fc5fd912dbd556c047cd4f9c2f88afbc9`. The operator identified a substantive
failure in the preceding review: the compulsory “strong objection” to amor fati
introduced an unargued moral premise about another person's unjust lot. It did
not engage Zarathustra's own treatment of pity. The earlier mechanical checks
and author review did not establish the adequacy of that interpretation.
The two older reviews below are retained as superseded records, not as approval
of the present edition or of the positions they described.

## What changed in the reading

The revised argument starts from a particular work and passage. Each textual
ground states which detail supports the proposed reading; a bibliography alone
is insufficient. Internal difficulties remain questions to examine in that
passage. An alternative reading is optional and has its own grounds. Neither
an objection nor an exercise is required to fill a card. Unargued personal
hypotheses belong in the user's notes. This remains authored demonstration
commentary, not a newly admitted source or a claim of philosophical consensus.

The review changed the substance of several organizing moves:

- Amor fati follows the movement of *The Gay Science* §276: a New Year wish,
  learning to see necessity as beautiful, making things beautiful, refusing
  accusation, looking away and the future task of becoming affirmative. The
  text is not turned into a ready method for acting on external circumstances.
- Pain, helping and affirmation return to *The Pitiful*, *The Sign*, *The Seven
  Seals* and *The Drunken Song* §§9–11. The readings retain pride, the shame of
  receiving help, creating love and Zarathustra's overcoming of pity for the
  higher humans. They do not substitute an unargued duty to compensate others
  for Nietzsche's question, or reduce every form of care to pity.
- The gateway questions, shepherd's bite and later convalescence are read in
  their actual narrative order. Questions about the shepherd interrupt the
  account before it reports the bite. The Convalescent's first section ends
  with disgust; collapse and seven days of illness begin its second section.
  The animals' final first-person formula is an imagined speech under the
  condition that Zarathustra wished to die now. It is not his spoken assent.
- Epictetus §1 retains its strong claims and the price of its distinction
  between one's own activity and externals. Spinoza's I, definition 7 concerns
  the source of determination; III.6–8 concern striving, actual essence and
  indefinite duration. Marcus Aurelius VI.44 proceeds through its three
  suppositions to rational and social nature and the benefit of two cities.
  An assumed conflict between personal and common benefit was removed.
- Kierkegaard is bounded by the supplied opening paragraph, including Diogenes,
  the proposed Berlin inquiry, opposed directions and its final qualification
  about taking time to live. The paragraph does not supply the trip's result.
  Heraclitus comparisons use the transmitted B12 and B30 units; unsupported
  appeals to B119 were removed. Leibniz §§53–55 concern possible universes and
  the reason for choosing one, not competing descriptions of one universe.
- Deleuze's two works remain separate: *Difference and Repetition*, introduction,
  printed pp.4–11, and *Nietzsche and Philosophy*, pp.49–52. Selection, movement
  in thought and the critique of the mechanical cycle are his interpretations.
  Camus is bounded by the concluding Sisyphus section: descent, consciousness,
  memory, fate and continuing effort. Neither comparison supplies a common
  ethical doctrine for all the thinkers in the atlas.

The older introductions, short relation explanations and route transitions were
examined with the new arguments. Revised prose is integrated into its authored
files; temporary proposal overlays and unused older node-body fallbacks are
removed from the source. This prevents an old premise surviving in the short
card while the expanded argument says something else. General instructions for
reading remain where they do useful methodological work.

# Semantic review of the philosophical walking demonstration

Review date: 2026-09-10. Subject: the authored demonstration changes in
`access/web/constructor/atlas-content.mjs`, `makeEdges()` in `atlas-data.mjs`,
and `atlas-routes.mjs`, based on commit
`344bba0a4cf880edaed10a8fbe44656789be2213`. Reviewers are the delegated content
and relation/route authors, with integration checks by the parent task. This is
an author review of a local product example, not independent scholarly review,
a ToS ledger event, source admission, canon, or approval to publish.

## What was examined

The node pass develops all 81 prepared materials in Russian and English:
seven source introductions remain separate from exact passages, and 74 demo
materials receive substantive explanations, perspectives, and questions or
examples where useful (131 perspectives, 18 examples and 29 questions across
the full overlay). The relation pass revises every retained explanation,
its endpoints and its type. The route pass follows eight questions through
actual graph relations, with 58 stops and 50 explained transitions spanning
46 distinct materials. The wider graph remains available for further reading.

The guiding correction is substantive: fate is explored through **received
conditions, a trajectory formed by actions, and retrospective interpretation**.
Agency can change conditions for later action without implying complete
control. Creation can concern forms, habits, values or possibilities; necessity
needs its causal, logical and retrospective senses distinguished. These are
explicit analytical proposals for the demonstration, not a single doctrine
attributed to Nietzsche or every comparison figure.

## Relation decisions

There are 128 authored relations: 19 `supports`, 48 `develops`, 16 `interprets`,
21 `questions`, 21 `compares`, and three `contrasts`. Each has a bilingual label
and an explanation of what connects the endpoints and what the connection
does not establish. In this fixture, support is a stated textual or conceptual
ground for a reading; development opens a further question; interpretation
applies a reading; a question challenges a definite difficulty; comparison
specifies shared and differing features. None of these types grants admission.

Five `contains` duplicates were removed from the authored atlas because the
library-owned source tree already supplies structure: `work → chapter-p3.r2`,
`work → chapter-p3.r13`, `chapter-p3.r2 → moment`, and
`chapter-p3.r13 → all-things / same-life`. Chapter links to the dwarf, shepherd,
animals and nausea now express textual support for demo material rather than
pretending those annotations are source-tree children.

Twenty-four relations retain their endpoints but change type. Their principal
corrections are:

- Identity/difference, cosmological/existential, poetic/cosmological,
  providence/cosmological, conatus/recurrence and possible-worlds/amor-fati
  become comparisons. Different questions or levels are not necessarily
  incompatible positions.
- Providence/necessity also becomes comparison: rational or purposive order
  adds something causal necessity alone does not entail.
- Images and concepts that generate an existing question use `develops`
  instead of suggesting they refute the question itself. This includes
  nausea/joy-pain, becoming/circle-change, decision/counterfactual,
  memory/remembered-return and attention/shared-world.
- The Stoic tradition develops an entry through Epictetus rather than
  containing a bibliographic object. Deleuze's difference relation becomes
  `interprets`, retaining its status as one later reading.
- `work → local-return` explicitly supports a demonstration paraphrase;
  `open-reading → character-author` develops an attribution question.

Two relations also change direction and type: `dossier interprets work`
replaces an unspecified work/dossier relation; `all-things supports animals`
restores the formula's textual speaker. The separate
`voice-reading interprets animals` edge concerns interpretive method and is
not a duplicate of speaker attribution.

Nine new links have particular work to do: `camus interprets fate` supplies a
primary-text entry; `sisyphus develops agency` opens a question about acting
under a continuing limit; `agency develops fate` and `creation develops fate`
connect actions with later conditions; `responsibility develops creation`
asks what a response can make; `joy-pain develops shared-world` and
`shared-world develops responsibility` retain others' independent perspectives;
`spinoza compares leibniz` specifies competing grounds of necessity; and
`camus develops memory` examines retrospective coherence. The Sisyphus/agency,
responsibility/creation and shared-world bridges are the atlas's own inquiries,
not claims that the named authors taught a common ethical system.

## Primary passages consulted

These links identify comparison evidence. They are not corpus witness records,
new quotations, or publication-rights determinations. Printed pages, sections
and the speaker's place matter more than a link's presence.

- Nietzsche, *Thus Spoke Zarathustra*, III, *The Vision and the Enigma* and
  *The Convalescent*: gateway questions, the dwarf's answer and Zarathustra's
  objection; shepherd, bite and laughter; animals' speech, disgust and the
  designation of the teacher. The English comparison text is Thomas Common's
  translation; its editorial commentary is not treated as Nietzsche's speech.
  [Primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/1998/pg1998-images.html).
  Source-library German/Russian bytes and provenance remain independently
  authoritative for the supplied passages.
- Nietzsche, *The Gay Science*, §276: the separate amor-fati entry.
  [German text](https://projekt-gutenberg.org/authors/friedrich-wilhelm-nietzsche/books/die-froehliche-wissenschaft/chapter/7/).
- Epictetus, *Enchiridion*, §§1, 8–9: one's judgments/actions, external conditions
  and one's relation to events. Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations*, VI.1, 7–11 and
  the connected whole: a specific Stoic setting, without equating all Stoics.
  [Epictetus](https://classics.mit.edu/Epictetus/epicench.html),
  [Marcus Aurelius](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.6.six.html).
- Spinoza, *Ethics*, I definition 7, I proposition 29 and the discussion after
  I proposition 33; III propositions 6–9; V proposition 6: necessity, conatus
  and the relation of understanding to affective dependence.
  [Primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/3800/3800-h/3800-h.htm).
- Leibniz, *Monadology*, §§53–57: possible worlds, sufficient reason, divine
  choice and perspective. Jonathan Bennett's translation visibly distinguishes
  its explanatory additions.
  [Primary text](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/leibniz1714b.pdf).
- Kierkegaard, *Repetition*, opening distinction of recollection/repetition,
  Constantin Constantius's Berlin experiment and the young man's letters/Job
  (PDF pages 25, 46–64 and 88–104, respectively, in the content author's
  inspection): retain their pseudonymous and religious differences. The PDF
  metadata title is misleading; the author checked the actual text.
  [French Tisseau translation](https://classiques.uqam.ca/classiques/Kierkegaard_Soren/La_repetition/Repetition.pdf).
- Camus, *The Myth of Sisyphus*, concluding essay, PDF page 49 (zero-based 48):
  the retrospective passage joins actions, memory and a fate made by the person.
  It grounds the bounded fate entry; broader practical bridges are this
  demonstration's proposals.
  [Primary text](https://thephilosopher.net/camus/wp-content/uploads/sites/94/2024/11/The-Myth-Of-Sisyphus-And-Other-Essays-Albert-Camus.pdf).
- Deleuze, the composite excerpt's *Nietzsche and Philosophy*, printed pp.49–52
  (zero-based PDF 10–13), and *Difference and Repetition*, introduction,
  printed pp.4–11 (PDF 21–24): difference, repetition and critique of the
  mechanical circle. These are Deleuze's interpretations, not neutral
  statements of a settled Nietzschean consensus.
  [Primary excerpts](https://blogs.law.columbia.edu/nietzsche1313/files/2016/11/Deleuze-complet.pdf).
- Heraclitus, transmitted river/opposition fragments, including B10/B12 and
  the opposed directions; the content pass also checked B119 and retained
  ambiguity around `daimon`. Different numbering and transmission remain visible.
  [Burnet compilation](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/ancient/heraklitos-fragments.asp),
  [Fragment collection used by the content pass](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Fragments_of_Heraclitus).
